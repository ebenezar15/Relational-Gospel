# Five research: fact checks (web research, 2026-10-26)
Counts are Greek NT lemma counts (NA27/28 via Mounce/Strong's lexicons) unless stated. Confidence: VERIFIED / PARTIAL / DISPUTED / NOT VERIFIED.

## A. Word counts

1. basileia, NT total. 162 (Greek). Mounce Greek Dictionary "frequency 162"; Wayne Jackson, Christian Courier. https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/basileia ; https://christiancourier.com/articles/a-brief-study-of-the-word-kingdom . VERIFIED.
   - Gospels: Matt 55, Mark 20, Luke 46, John 5 (= 126). These are standard concordance tallies, but no web source checked them per book. PARTIAL. John 5 = 3:3, 3:5, 18:36 x3.
   - "Kingdom of heaven" (basileia ton ouranon): 32x, Matthew only. Several sources, incl. Concordia Theological Monthly 1950. https://scholar.csl.edu/ctm/vol21/iss1/2 . VERIFIED. Matthew also says "kingdom of God" about 4-5x (12:28; 19:24; 21:31, 43; 6:33 in some MSS).
   - "Kingdom of God": ~65-68 in Greek NT (Mark 14, Luke 32, Matt 4-5, John 2, Acts 6, Paul ~8, Rev 1). PARTIAL. Published totals vary widely (70; 122; "135 incl. heaven/Christ") because they count the phrase differently. https://www.catholic.com/magazine/online-edition/what-is-the-kingdom-of-god ; https://academic.logos.com/?p=6873 . Give the figure as "about 65" and name the counting method.

2. euangelion 76x, euangelizo 54x. Mounce gives 54 for the verb; Tyndale NLT gives 76 for the noun. One dissertation counts 77 and 55 (MS variants / what is counted). https://www.billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/euangelizo ; https://sites.tyndale.com/nlt/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/5961 . VERIFIED (with +/-1 caveat).
   - "Father" for God on Jesus' lips: Mark 4, Luke 15, Matthew 42, John 109 (total ~170). Joachim Jeremias, The Prayers of Jesus (1967), pp. 29-36 (also in The Central Message of the NT). The ~170 total and Mark 4 are confirmed online (Ligonier; CT "Abba" article). Other secondary sources give "Matthew ~30, John 120+" (different criteria). https://learn.ligonier.org/articles/our-father . PARTIAL. Cite as Jeremias's count. His wider "Abba" thesis ("Daddy") is DISPUTED (James Barr, "Abba isn't Daddy", JTS 1988).

3. Teaching-emphasis claims:
   - "16 of 38 parables are about money/possessions" (also "11 of 39", "a third", "Jesus talked about money more than heaven and hell"). This appears only in stewardship and ministry material, with no stated method. A fact-check (Thinking to Believe, 2023) says the figure counts parables that mention money, not parables about money; by the same logic, food appears in 18 parables. https://thinkingtobelieve.com/2023/08/22/fact-check-jesus-talked-about-money-more-than-any-other-topic/ . NOT VERIFIED / treat as MYTH-ish. Safer wording: "Luke gives sustained attention to wealth (Rich Fool, Unjust Steward, Rich Man and Lazarus)" (D.J. Weaver, Baylor IFL). https://ifl.web.baylor.edu/sites/g/files/ecbvkj771/files/2022-11/ParablesArticleWeaver.pdf
   - "1 in 7 verses of Luke is about money". No source found. NOT VERIFIED; avoid.
   - Healing as ~1/5 of the Gospels: Morton Kelsey (Healing and Christianity, 1973) is quoted in secondary sources as "nearly one-fifth of the Gospels" and 41 distinct healing accounts. The verse count ("727 verses") was not found. https://www.spiritualityandpractice.com/books/reviews/view/2681 . PARTIAL (one author's estimate).
   - Number of parables: counts range from ~30 to 60+ depending on whether sayings or similitudes are included. Jeremias treats ~41; Snodgrass, Stories with Intent (2008), treats ~33 and says about one-third of Jesus' Synoptic teaching is in parables. PARTIAL (no single number is "correct"). Kingdom parables: no standard count. Matthew 13 has 7 (8 counting 13:52); about 10 in Matthew open with "the kingdom of heaven is like". PARTIAL.

4. agape 116x, agapao 143x (Mounce lexicon frequencies; not re-checked online). PARTIAL.
   - Gehenna 12x: 11 in the Synoptics, 1 in James 3:6. https://saintebible.com/greek/1067.htm ; Encyclopedia of the Bible (BibleGateway). VERIFIED. All 11 Synoptic uses are in Jesus' speech (Matt 5:22,29,30; 10:28; 18:9; 23:15,33; Mark 9:43,45,47; Luke 12:5), so "11 from Jesus" holds. Per-Gospel this is 7 + 3 + 1; parallels overlap, so the number of distinct sayings is fewer. VERIFIED.

5. Great Commission texts: the usual five are Matt 28:18-20; Mark 16:15 (longer ending, textually disputed, so flag it); Luke 24:46-49; John 20:21; Acts 1:8. Allen Yeh (Biola) in Barna's Q&A: "five passages are commonly treated as Great Commissions"; the phrase itself is not biblical. https://access.barna.com/articles/future-teaching-great-commission . VERIFIED.
   - Paul and congregational evangelism. Robert L. Plummer, Paul's Understanding of the Church's Mission (Paternoster, 2006). Thesis: the gospel is a dynamic, self-spreading word, so the churches inherit the apostolic mission. Evidence: Phil 1:12-18; Eph 6:15-17 (active); Col 4:5-6; 1 Thess 4:12; 1 Cor 10:31-11:1 (passive/apologetic). Reviewers concede that "Paul rarely explicitly commands" churches to evangelize. Plummer acknowledges there is no consensus. https://baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/paul-expected-churches-to-evangelize-prof-says-in-new-book ; 9Marks review https://www.9marks.org/?p=4333 . VERIFIED.
   - Opposing or minimalist view: W. Paul Bowers ("Church and Mission in Paul", JSNT 1991), David Bosch, and John P. Dickson. Dickson, Mission-Commitment in Ancient Judaism and in the Pauline Communities (WUNT 2/159, Mohr Siebeck 2003) argues that Paul expected congregations to promote the gospel mainly by prayer, financial support, godly conduct and apologetic speech, while "gospelling" proper belonged to commissioned heralds. His popular book is Promoting the Gospel (2005; US title The Best Kept Secret of Christian Mission, 2010). PARTIAL; the position comes from reviews, and the book itself was not fetched.

## B. History of evangelism emphasis

6. Carey, An Enquiry into the Obligations of Christians to use Means for the Conversion of the Heathens (Leicester, 1792). Section I: "An Enquiry whether the Commission given by our Lord to his Disciples be not still binding on us." Carey rebuts the view that the commission ended with the apostles. VERIFIED.
   - "Sit down, young man... when God pleases to convert the heathen, He will do it without your aid or mine" (attributed to John Ryland Sr., 1785/86). DISPUTED/apocryphal. Ryland Jr., who was present, said he never heard it until he saw it in print (Iain Murray, The Puritan Hope, 1971). The earliest account (George Smith, Life of William Carey, 1885) frames the objection as "nothing can be done before another Pentecost". https://bredenhof.ca/2010/10/01/perpetuating-falsehoods-in-the-history-of-mission/ ; https://www.ccel.org/ccel/smith_geo/carey.iv.html .
   - "Reformers thought the commission was for the apostles only": PARTIAL / overstated. Theodore Beza and Lutheran orthodoxy (Johann Gerhard) are cited (Gustav Warneck, 1882) for "the commission was fulfilled by the apostles". Scholars such as Scott Hendrix, and Bredenhof on the Belgic Confession, argue the Reformers had a real missionary theology. Hyper-Calvinist reluctance in 18th-c. English Particular Baptist circles is better attested. Present this as debated.
   - The phrase "Great Commission": possibly first used by Justinian von Welz (Lutheran, 1660s); popularized by Hudson Taylor in the later 19th c. Wikipedia says "not known who coined" it; Themelios: "it was Hudson Taylor, nearly 200 years later, who popularized" it; Lausanne: popular use is "no older than a hundred and fifty years". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Commission ; https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/article/the-last-word-the-great-commission-ecclesiology/ . PARTIAL (popularization VERIFIED; coinage uncertain). The Taylor quote "The Great Commission is not an option to be considered..." has no source and is likely apocryphal.

7. Bebbington quadrilateral. D.W. Bebbington, Evangelicalism in Modern Britain: A History from the 1730s to the 1980s (Unwin Hyman, 1989), pp. 2-3: "conversionism, the belief that lives need to be changed; activism, the expression of the gospel in effort; biblicism, a particular regard for the Bible; and what may be called crucicentrism, a stress on the sacrifice of Christ on the cross." VERIFIED (standard quotation).

8. Revivalist methods:
   - Finney: "new measures" (anxious bench/seat, protracted meetings, women praying aloud), systematized in Lectures on Revivals of Religion (1835). The anxious seat was used prominently at Rochester, 1830-31. Lecture 1: a revival "is not a miracle... It is a purely philosophical result of the right use of the constituted means." VERIFIED. The anxious bench predates Finney (Methodist mourner's bench; Wheelock reported 1741). Bennett (summarized by 9Marks): Finney did not use a set "sinner's prayer". https://www.9marks.org/review/the-origins-of-the-altar-call-and-the-sinners-prayer/
   - Moody: "I look upon this world as a wrecked vessel. God has given me a lifeboat and said to me, 'Moody, save all you can.'" From the sermon "Our Lord's Return" (variant "take the lifeboat and save as many as you can"), in New Sermons, Addresses and Prayers (1877). Year from secondary sources only. https://e-n.org.uk/features/2007-11-save-all-you-can . PARTIAL.
   - Four Spiritual Laws: CORRECTION on date. Most sources (Baptist Press obituary, Cru) say Bright wrote it in 1956 (some say 1952). It was published as the booklet "Have You Heard of the Four Spiritual Laws?" in 1965. Distribution: "more than 2.5 billion" (Cru/Baptist Press), "most widely disseminated religious booklet in history". These are organization figures. https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/campus-crusade-founder-bill-bright-dies-four-spiritual-laws-among-legacies/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Bright . PARTIAL (write 1956 / 1965).
   - Evangelism Explosion (D. James Kennedy, Coral Ridge, 1962; incorporated 1972). Q1: "Have you come to a place in your spiritual life where you know for certain that if you were to die tonight you would go to heaven?" Q2: "If you were to die tonight and stand before God and He were to say to you, 'Why should I let you into my heaven?' what would you say?" Q2 was adapted from Donald Grey Barnhouse (CT, 1969). https://christianitytoday.com/news/2007/september/136-43.0.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism_Explosion . VERIFIED.
   - Sinner's prayer: a set-form prayer is modern. Earliest printed example found by David Bennett, The Altar Call (2000): 1922, Albert Gage, Evangelism of Youth. Billy Sunday's meetings popularized it; Graham's Peace with God / "Steps to Peace with God" tract and Bright's Four Laws spread it. Paul Chitwood (dissertation, SBTS 2001) dates it to the early 20th c. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinner%27s_prayer . PARTIAL.
   - Billy Graham: "nearly 215 million people in more than 185 countries and territories" in live audiences (BGEA). This counts satellite-linked live audiences (Global Mission 1995 etc.); in-person crusade attendance was ~84 million (110 million with other events) per NCR obituary 2018. https://billygraham.org/?p=13787 ; https://www.ncronline.org/node/161951 . PARTIAL. Say "live audiences, including satellite".

9. Barna:
   - Translating the Great Commission (Barna/Seed Company, 2018): 51% of US churchgoers do not know the term "Great Commission"; 25% have heard it but can't recall the meaning; 17% know it and what it means; 6% unsure. Millennials: only 10% know it. https://www.barna.com/research/half-churchgoers-not-heard-great-commission/ . VERIFIED.
   - Reviving Evangelism (Barna/Alpha USA, 2019): "almost half of Millennials (47%) agree at least somewhat that it is wrong to share one's personal beliefs with someone of a different faith in hopes that they will one day share the same faith". This is the practicing-Christian Millennial sample (Gen X 27%, Boomers 19%, Elders 20%). In the same study 96% (95-97%) say being a witness is part of faith, and 73% feel gifted at sharing it. https://www.barna.com/research/millenials-oppose-evangelism/ . VERIFIED. Correction: the statement is about sharing with someone "of a different faith", not sharing faith in general.
   - Shared faith in the past year: Barna (2013) found 52% of born-again Christians shared their faith with someone of different beliefs in the past year. Barna 2025 trend: 61% of US Christians feel personal responsibility to share faith, but only 28% strongly; "one in three" strongly (Dec 2025 piece; definitions vary). https://www.barna.com/trends/faiths-shrinking-influence/ . PARTIAL (check the exact year before citing).

## C. Kingdom of God background

10. Priene Calendar Inscription (OGIS 458; IK Priene 14; 9 BC; decree of the Asian koinon adopting Paullus Fabius Maximus's edict). The new year begins on Augustus's birthday (23 Sept). Key lines (tr. after Danker/Evans): Providence "sent us and those after us a saviour (soter) who put an end to war and set all things in order... and since the birthday of the god (he genethlios hemera tou theou) was the beginning for the world of the good tidings (euangelia) that came by reason of him..." Greek: erxen de toi kosmoi ton di' auton euangelion he genethlios hemera tou theou. Note: euangelion is plural here, and the text is partly restored; one encyclopedia notes it could mean "thank-offerings for good news". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priene_calendar_inscription ; Craig A. Evans, "Mark's Incipit and the Priene Calendar Inscription", JGRChJ 1 (2000) 67-81. VERIFIED (with restoration caveat).
   - Imperial titles: divi filius / theou huios ("son of the deified one/god"); soter (saviour); kyrios (lord) used for emperors from Nero onward in papyri (Deissmann, Light from the Ancient East); Augustus refused "dominus" in Rome. VERIFIED (general scholarship; Deissmann 1908/1927).

11. Jewish expectation:
   - "Kingdom of heaven" as a reverential circumlocution (Dalman, Die Worte Jesu, 1898): this traditional view is now DISPUTED. Jonathan Pennington, Heaven and Earth in the Gospel of Matthew (Brill 2007; Baker 2009), shows Matthew uses "God" freely (~50x) and argues "heaven" serves a heaven/earth contrast theme. Present the circumlocution view as traditional and contested.
   - Kaddish: "May he establish his kingdom in your lifetime and in your days and in the lifetime of all the house of Israel, swiftly and soon." VERIFIED as text. Dating of the wording is debated; Jesus' Lord's Prayer parallel ("hallowed be your name; your kingdom come") is widely noted (Jeremias, Meier, A Marginal Jew vol. 2). Core may be 1st c.; written attestation is later (Seder Rav Amram, 9th c.). PARTIAL on date.
   - Psalms of Solomon 17 (mid-1st c. BC, after Pompey 63 BC): 17:21 "See, Lord, and raise up for them their king, the son of David..."; 17:32 "and their king shall be the Lord Messiah" (christos kyrios; some emend to christos kyriou). VERIFIED (OTP vol. 2, R.B. Wright tr.).
   - Two ages (olam ha-zeh / olam ha-ba): m. Avot 4:16 "This world is like a vestibule before the world to come"; 4 Ezra 7:50 "The Most High has made not one world but two"; NT: Matt 12:32; Mark 10:30; Eph 1:21. VERIFIED.
   - Dan 2:44 (God sets up a kingdom that "shall never be destroyed"); Dan 7:13-14 ("one like a son of man"... "dominion, glory and kingdom"). VERIFIED (text).

12. Millennial views:
   - Postmillennial: Puritan "latter-day glory" (Iain Murray, The Puritan Hope, 1971); Jonathan Edwards, A History of the Work of Redemption (sermons 1739, publ. 1774) and An Humble Attempt (1747); Princeton (Charles Hodge, A.A. Hodge, B.B. Warfield) broadly postmillennial. VERIFIED (standard histories).
   - Amillennial: Augustine, City of God XX.7-9: the thousand years = the church age; "the Church even now is the kingdom of Christ, and the kingdom of heaven" (XX.9). VERIFIED.
   - Dispensational postponement: kingdom offered to Israel, rejected, postponed, so the church age is a "parenthesis". Sources: Scofield Reference Bible (1909/1917) notes on Matt 4:17 and 13; L.S. Chafer, The Kingdom in History and Prophecy (1915; chs. "The Kingdom Offered", "The Kingdom Rejected and Postponed"); J. Dwight Pentecost, Things to Come (1958); Thy Kingdom Come (1990); Alva McClain, The Greatness of the Kingdom (1959). Progressive dispensationalists (Blaising & Bock 1993; Saucy 1993) soften or reject strict postponement. https://tms.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/09/tmsj7h.pdf ; https://faithlife.com/store/product/15142 . VERIFIED.

## D. Prophecy and reception

13. Conditional prophecy: Jer 18:7-10 sets out the principle (announced judgment is reversed if the nation repents; announced blessing is withdrawn if it does evil). Examples: Jonah 3:4, 10 (Nineveh: "forty days" not fulfilled); 2 Kgs 20:1-6 / Isa 38 (Hezekiah given 15 years); Mic 3:12 with Jer 26:18-19. Scholarly summaries: Robert B. Chisholm Jr., "When Prophecy Appears to Fail, Check Your Hermeneutic", JETS 53.3 (2010) 561-77; Richard L. Pratt Jr., "Historical Contingencies and Biblical Predictions" (in The Way of Wisdom, 2000); Walter Brueggemann and others on Jer 18. VERIFIED texts; PARTIAL on bibliographic details.

14. Pentecostal/charismatic numbers:
   - Pew (2011, using Center for the Study of Global Christianity data for 2010): 279 million pentecostals + 305 million charismatics = 584 million, ~27% of Christians, ~8% of world population. Pew notes these are mostly church-reported numbers. https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2011/12/19/global-christianity-movements-and-denominations/ . VERIFIED.
   - Johnson & Zurlo, Introducing Spirit-Empowered Christianity (2020; Gordon-Conwell CSGC): ~644 million "Spirit-empowered" in 2020 (~26% of Christians), including independent charismatics and African Independent and Chinese house churches. https://religionnews.com/2020/02/25/spirit-empowered-christianity-is-one-of-the-fastest-growing-global-movements-new-study-shows/ . VERIFIED. Definitions differ, so the series are not strictly comparable. More recent CSGC "Status of Global Christianity" tables (2024-25) put it near ~680-700 million (NOT VERIFIED here).

15. Ministers and moral failure (aggregate data only):
   - Lifeway Research (2015, evangelical and historically Black churches). Reasons predecessors left the pastorate: change of calling 37%, family issues 17%, poor fit 13%, moral or ethical issues 13%. Only ~1% of senior pastors leave the pastorate per year. https://christianitytoday.com/news/2015/september/one-percent-why-so-few-evangelical-pastors-quit-lifeway.html . VERIFIED.
   - Lifeway Research (2025, former senior pastors in four denominations): change of calling 40%, church conflict 18%, burnout 16%, moral/ethical issues only 3%. https://religionunplugged.com/news/2025/8/12/former-pastors-share-reasons-behind-their-ministry-exit . VERIFIED. The two studies used different samples, so don't present 13% to 3% as a trend.
   - Barna / Josh McDowell, The Porn Phenomenon (2016): 14% of pastors and 21% of youth pastors say they currently struggle with porn; 57% and 64% have struggled currently or in the past; 5% and 12% say they are addicted. Self-reported; n = 432 pastors, 338 youth pastors. https://christianitytoday.com/news/2016/january/how-pastors-struggle-porn-phenomenon-josh-mcdowell-barna.html . VERIFIED.
   - Avoid widely circulated "1,500 pastors leave every month" / "70% of pastors..." statistics (Schaeffer Institute/Krejcir). Lifeway's own analysis contradicts them, and they are DISPUTED/unreliable.
