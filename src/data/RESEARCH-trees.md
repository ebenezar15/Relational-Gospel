# Two Trees: verified dates, sources and survey data

Compiled 2026-10-24. Confidence labels: VERIFIED (primary source or several reliable sources agree), PARTIAL (the core is right but a detail is off or contested), DISPUTED (credible sources disagree), NOT VERIFIED (no adequate source found).
Method: WebSearch, plus Apify web-fetch for full text (primary PDFs and pages). WebFetch and direct curl were blocked (DNS and proxy). Firecrawl had no credits.

## A. Survey data: Ligonier / Lifeway "State of Theology"

**Key methodological note.** "Evangelical" here means *evangelical by belief*: the respondent strongly agrees with all four NAE/Lifeway statements (Bible is highest authority; it is very important to encourage non-Christians to trust Christ; Christ's death is the only sacrifice that removes sin's penalty; only those who trust Christ alone receive salvation). It does **not** mean self-identified evangelicals.

1. **Latest wave is 2025, not 2022.**
   - Figure: 3,001 US adults, online panel, fielded Jan 6–15, 2025, margin of error ±1.9%. This is the 6th wave; earlier waves were 2014, 2016, 2018, 2020 and 2022. The report was released Sept 2025.
   - Source: Lifeway Research, *2025 State of American Theology Study* white paper (2025).
   - URL: https://research.lifeway.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/Ligonier-State-of-Theology-2025-White-Paper.pdf
   - Confidence: VERIFIED (primary)

2. **"Everyone sins a little, but most people are good by nature."**
   - 2025: **53%** of evangelicals agree (Ligonier); 43% of evangelicals disagree (white paper). **66%** of all US adults agree, against 67% in 2014, so the figure has been stable across every wave.
   - 2022 evangelicals: **55%** (The Gospel Coalition, TGC) or **57%** (Christianity Today, CT). The two outlets disagree.
   - Sources: Ligonier, "The Results from Our 2025 State of Theology Survey Are In" (Sept 19, 2025); 2025 white paper p.13; TGC 2022; CT 2022.
   - URLs: https://www.ligonier.org/posts/the-results-from-our-2025-state-of-theology-survey-are-in ; https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/state-theology-2022/ ; https://www.christianitytoday.com/2022/09/state-of-theology-evangelical-heresy-report-ligonier-survey/
   - Confidence: VERIFIED for 2025. DISPUTED (55 vs 57) for 2022.

3. **"Everyone is born innocent in the eyes of God."**
   - 2025: **74%** of all US adults agree, up from 71% in 2022. Evangelicals: 31% disagree. TGC's 2025 write-up says **64%** of evangelicals affirm it.
   - Source: 2025 white paper p.14; TGC 2025.
   - URL: as in #1 ; https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/evangelical-2025-theology-survey/
   - Confidence: VERIFIED for all adults. PARTIAL for the evangelical 64%, which is secondary but consistent with the 31% who disagree.

4. **"Jesus is the first and greatest being created by God."**
   - 2022: **73%** of evangelicals agree. TGC and CT agree on this figure. In 2014, 19% of all Americans said Jesus was the first creature made by God.
   - **This item does not appear in the 2025 survey.** The 2022 figure is the latest available.
   - Sources: TGC 2022; CT 2022; Lifeway 2014 release.
   - URLs: https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/article/state-theology-2022/ ; https://www.christianitytoday.com/2022/09/state-of-theology-evangelical-heresy-report-ligonier-survey/ ; https://news.lifeway.com/2014/10/28/new-research-americans-believe-in-heaven-hell-and-a-little-bit-of-heresy/
   - Confidence: VERIFIED for 2022 (secondary, but two outlets agree). Note that it was not re-asked.

5. **"God accepts the worship of all religions, including Christianity, Judaism, and Islam."**
   - 2025: **47%** of evangelicals agree (Ligonier); 45% of evangelicals disagree; **65%** of all adults agree.
   - 2022 evangelicals: **56%** (CT, up from 42% in 2020) or **58%** (TGC).
   - Sources: Ligonier 2025; white paper p.8; CT 2022; TGC 2022.
   - Confidence: VERIFIED for 2025. DISPUTED (56 vs 58) for 2022.

6. **"Religious belief is a matter of personal opinion; it is not about objective truth."**
   - 2022: **38%** of evangelicals agree, against 23% in 2020 (CT).
   - 2025: the wording was shortened to "Religious belief is not about objective truth." **46%** of all adults agree and 53% of evangelicals disagree. No evangelical "agree" figure is published.
   - Sources: CT 2022; white paper p.23.
   - Confidence: VERIFIED for 2022 (secondary) and for the 2025 overall figure. Do not compare 2022 and 2025 directly, because the wording changed.

7. **"Jesus was a great teacher, but he was not God."**
   - 2022 evangelicals: **43%** (CT) or **44%** (TGC).
   - 2025: **49%** of all adults agree, down from 53% in 2022. 68% of evangelicals disagree, so at most about 32% agree.
   - Source: white paper p.10.
   - Confidence: VERIFIED. There is an apparent sharp evangelical improvement since 2022, but no exact 2025 evangelical agree-percentage is published.

8. **"The Holy Spirit is a force but is not a personal being."**
   - 2025: **53%** of evangelicals agree (Ligonier); **57%** of all adults agree.
   - 2022 evangelicals: 60% (CT) or 55% (TGC).
   - 2014: 59% of evangelicals (denominational definition) and 64% of all Americans.
   - Confidence: VERIFIED for 2025. DISPUTED for 2022.

9. **"Even the smallest sin deserves eternal damnation."**
   - 2025: **23%** of all adults agree; **49%** of evangelicals agree (against 16% of non-evangelicals). 2014: 18% of all adults.
   - 2022: 53% of evangelicals *disagreed* (TGC).
   - Source: white paper p.13; TGC 2022.
   - Confidence: VERIFIED

10. **"Hell is a real place where certain people will be punished forever."**
    - 2025: **57%** of all adults; **94%** of evangelicals. The 2014 figures were 61% of all adults and 87% of evangelicals (denominational definition).
    - Source: white paper p.17; Lifeway 2014.
    - Confidence: VERIFIED

11. **Other useful 2025 items (all adults, from the white paper):**
    - God loves all people the same way: 83%
    - Trinity: 71%
    - God counts a person righteous by faith, not works: 56% (evangelicals 88%)
    - God chose the people he would save before creation: 26% (evangelicals 41%)
    - Jesus will return to judge all people: 62% (evangelicals 98%)
    - Confidence: VERIFIED

12. **2014 baseline (Lifeway for Ligonier).**
    - 3,000 adults, Feb 25–Mar 5, 2014.
    - 71% say people must contribute some effort toward their own salvation.
    - 64% say people must take the first step and God then responds with grace.
    - 67% say most people are basically good.
    - URL: https://news.lifeway.com/2014/10/28/new-research-americans-believe-in-heaven-hell-and-a-little-bit-of-heresy/
    - Confidence: VERIFIED (primary)

## B. Mental health and the church

13. **"48% of evangelicals believe prayer and Bible study alone can overcome serious mental illness."**
    - The study is from **2013, not 2014**: a Lifeway telephone survey of 1,001 adults, Sept 6–10, 2013.
    - Statement tested: "With just Bible study and prayer, ALONE, people with serious mental illness like depression, bipolar disorder, and schizophrenia could overcome mental illness."
    - Results: **35%** of all Americans agree. **48%** of "evangelical, fundamentalist, or born-again Christians" agree, a self-identified group. 27% of others agree. 50% of 18–29-year-olds agree.
    - Ed Stetzer: "In a typical evangelical church, half the people believe mental illness can be solved by prayer and Bible study alone."
    - Source: Bob Smietana, Lifeway, Sept 17, 2013.
    - URL: https://news.lifeway.com/2013/09/17/half-of-evangelicals-believe-prayer-can-heal-mental-illness/
    - Confidence: VERIFIED. The usual citation is wrong in two ways: the year (2013, not 2014) and the population. The 48% is self-identified evangelicals, not "churchgoers".

14. **2014 Lifeway / Focus on the Family study on mental illness (released Sept 2014).** The study had three groups:
    - 1,000 senior Protestant pastors
    - 355 Protestants with acute mental illness (200 of them churchgoers)
    - 207 family members

    Findings:
    - **66%** of pastors speak about mental illness once a year or less.
    - **49%** speak about it rarely (39%) or never (10%).
    - 16% speak about it once a year.
    - **22%** are reluctant to help people with acute mental illness because it takes too much time.
    - Only **27%** of churches have a plan to help affected families, and only 21% of family members know of one.
    - 14% of churches have a skilled counselor and 13% train leaders.
    - 68% of pastors say their church keeps a list of mental health resources, but only 28% of families know it exists.
    - 65% of family members and 59% of those with mental illness want the church to talk openly about it.
    - **23%** of pastors have personally struggled with mental illness, about half of them diagnosed (CT).

    Sources and confidence:
    - Lifeway Research; CT, Sept 22, 2014.
    - URLs: https://research.lifeway.com/?p=1753 ; https://www.christianitytoday.com/2014/09/1-in-4-pastors-have-mental-illness-lifeway-focus-on-family/ ; https://religionnews.com/2014/09/22/churches-shun-mental-illness-offer-little-help-sufferers-families/
    - VERIFIED. The 66% and 49% figures are not contradictory: 49% plus the 16% "once a year" gives roughly 66%.

15. **Pastors' mental health, later data.**
    - Lifeway 2021: **26%** of Protestant pastors have personally struggled with mental illness (17% diagnosed, 9% undiagnosed).
    - Barna, March 2022: **42%** of pastors had seriously considered quitting full-time ministry in the past year, up from 38% in fall 2021. Of those, 56% cite "the immense stress of the job" and 43% loneliness and isolation. This is a burnout measure, not a diagnosis.
    - URLs: https://research.lifeway.com/?p=120865 ; https://www.barna.com/?p=1162 ; https://christianpost.com/news/more-pastors-now-say-they-have-considered-quitting-study.html
    - Confidence: VERIFIED (Lifeway 26%, Barna 42%). The widely shared "51% of pastors have suffered depression" (attributed to *The Resilient Pastor*) is NOT VERIFIED.

16. **Religious trauma prevalence: Slade, Smell, Wilson & Drumsta (2023).**
    - Publication: "Percentage of U.S. Adults Suffering from Religious Trauma: A Sociological Study," *Socio-Historical Examination of Religion and Ministry* (SHERM) 5/1 (2023): 1–28, doi:10.33929/sherm.2023.vol5.no1.01. The fourth author is Rebekah Drumsta.
    - Sample: n = 1,581 US adults.
    - Abstract: "around one-third (27‒33%) of U.S. adults (conservatively) have experienced religious trauma at some point in their life. That number increases to 37% if those suffering from any three of the six major RT symptoms are included … around 10‒15% … currently suffer … as many as one-in-five (20%)."
    - Caveats: the study was published by the Global Center for Religious Research, which is also the first author's own affiliation and the journal's publisher. It is not a probability sample in a mainstream peer-reviewed psychology journal, and critics (e.g. the Catholic League) dispute it.
    - URL: https://ia801603.us.archive.org/3/items/sherm.2023.vol5.no1.01/Percentage%20of%20U.S.%20Adults%20Suffering%20from%20Religious%20Trauma.pdf
    - Confidence: VERIFIED that the paper says this. Treat it as one estimate, not settled fact.

17. **Assurance of salvation / fear of hell among evangelicals.**
    - No Lifeway, Barna or Pew figure was found for the share of evangelicals who "worry about their salvation."
    - The closest item: Lifeway 2014 noted that "Americans don't seem too worried about sin or being sent to hell" (an interpretive line, not a measured percentage).
    - Confidence: NOT VERIFIED. Do not cite a percentage.

## C. Historical origins and dates (Tree 1: Western / Reformed / evangelical)

18. **Augustine on original sin and inherited guilt.**
    - *City of God* XIII.14 (written c. 413–426): "we all were in that one man … man, being of his own will corrupted, and justly condemned, begot corrupted and condemned children." The *Enchiridion* (c. 420–421), §26–27, teaches the same "condemned mass" idea.
    - URLs: https://www.logoslibrary.org/augustine/city/1314.html ; https://bkv.unifr.ch/works/cpl-313/versions/the-city-of-god/divisions/379
    - Confidence: VERIFIED for the *City of God* text. The *Enchiridion* section numbers come from standard knowledge and were not re-fetched (PARTIAL).

19. **Romans 5:12, "in quo" vs. Greek *eph' hō*.**
    - The Latin "in quo omnes peccaverunt" ("in whom all sinned") was read by Augustine as "in Adam." Most modern scholars of all confessions read *eph' hō* as "because" (Meyendorff, *Byzantine Theology*, Fordham 1974, pp. 143–145, quoted secondhand).
    - **Nuance:** the "in quo" reading predates Augustine. It appears in the Latin Origen (Rufinus) and in Ambrosiaster. It was standard in the Old Latin/Vulgate tradition, not Augustine's invention. Scholars such as G. Bonner (1968) argue that Augustine's doctrine does not rest on this verse alone.
    - URLs: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/new-testament-studies/article/consecutive-meaning-of-e-in-romans-512/ADC4270FE5F666EE1B093D2C7E2F0385 ; https://catenabible.com/rom/5/12
    - Confidence: PARTIAL. The translation point is solid. The claim that "the mistranslation created original sin" is an overstatement.

20. **Council (Synod) of Carthage, 418.**
    - Met May 1, 418 under Aurelius against Caelestius and Pelagius. Its canons condemned the denial of original sin, affirmed infant baptism "for remission of sins," and affirmed the necessity of grace. Pope Zosimus then issued the *Epistola tractoria*.
    - The "Canon 110" numbering comes from the 419 *Codex canonum ecclesiae Africanae* compilation.
    - URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Councils_of_Carthage ; https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/dailystory/permalink/people-cannot-save-themselves-by-good-deeds-said-african-bishops
    - Confidence: VERIFIED

21. **Anselm, *Cur Deus Homo*.**
    - Begun in England, finished in exile in Italy ("Capua" region; the preface says "in Capuana provincia"). Usually dated 1094–1098, completed **1098**. It presents the satisfaction theory of atonement (honour and debt), not yet penal substitution as such.
    - URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cur_Deus_Homo
    - Confidence: VERIFIED (completed 1098)

22. **Calvin, *Institutes*.**
    - First Latin edition Basel 1536. Latin editions in 1536, 1539, 1543, 1550 and **1559** (final and definitive, Geneva). French editions in 1541 and **1560**. English in 1561.
    - URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institutes_of_the_Christian_Religion
    - Confidence: VERIFIED

23. **Synod of Dort, 1618–19.**
    - Opened 13 Nov 1618. The Canons were adopted April 1619 (sources give Apr 22/23). The synod closed May 1619; sources differ between May 6 and May 29 because they count either the international sessions or the final Dutch-only sessions.
    - It answered the five articles of the 1610 Remonstrance. The order of the Canons is Election, Atonement, Depravity, Grace, Perseverance, not T-U-L-I-P.
    - URLs: https://frcna.org/canons-of-dort ; https://christianitytoday.com/history/2019/december/synod-dort-reformer-arminianism-tulip.html
    - Confidence: VERIFIED. The exact closing date is DISPUTED.

24. **TULIP acronym, c. 1905 at the earliest.**
    - The earliest documented use is William H. Vail in *The Outlook* (1913). He recalls a lecture by Cleland Boyd McAfee "some eight years ago," hence c. 1905. In McAfee's version, "U" stood for "universal sovereignty."
    - Loraine Boettner (*The Reformed Doctrine of Predestination*, 1932) popularized it with U = unconditional election.
    - URLs: https://thisday.pcahistory.org/2013/09/originator-of-the-t-u-l-i-p-acrostic/ ; https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/blogs/justin-taylor/tweaking-the-tulip/
    - Confidence: VERIFIED. The c. 1905 date is inferred from Vail's wording.

25. **Westminster Confession of Faith.**
    - The Assembly sat from 1643. The Confession was completed and sent to Parliament in late **1646**. The Commons required Scripture proofs, which were added in spring 1647. The Church of Scotland General Assembly approved it on Aug 27, 1647. The English Parliament adopted a modified version in 1648.
    - Ch. 6: the Fall brings guilt imputed to all posterity. Ch. 8: Christ's death "fully satisfied the justice of his Father."
    - URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_Confession_of_Faith ; https://bcw-project.org.uk/church-and-state/first-civil-war/westminster-assembly
    - Confidence: VERIFIED (1646 completion). The specific "4 December 1646" date is not re-confirmed (PARTIAL).

26. **Hugo Grotius, governmental theory (1617).**
    - Work: *Defensio fidei catholicae de satisfactione Christi adversus Faustum Socinum* (1617). God is pictured as moral governor and the penalty is "relaxed" for the common good.
    - Recent scholarship (Hoogenboom 2023; *First Things*) argues that Grotius himself stayed close to Reformed satisfaction. The full "governmental theory" label comes more from later Arminian and New England developments.
    - URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governmental_theory_of_atonement ; https://firstthings.com/is-grotius-a-grotian
    - Confidence: VERIFIED for the date and work. DISPUTED on whether Grotius was a "Grotian."

27. **Charles Hodge (1797–1878).**
    - Taught at Princeton Seminary for about 50 years. *Systematic Theology*, 3 vols, **1871–73** (some sources say 1872–73). It is the classic American statement of penal substitution and imputed guilt. "Taught over 3,000 students" rests on a single source.
    - URLs: https://ccel.org/ccel/hodge/theology3 ; https://learn.ligonier.org/podcasts/5-minutes-in-church-history-with-stephen-nichols/hodges-systematic-theology
    - Confidence: VERIFIED for the dates. PARTIAL for the 3,000 figure.

28. **J. N. Darby and dispensationalism (1830s).**
    - Darby took part in the Powerscourt conferences of 1831–33, and the conventional account says he taught the pre-tribulation rapture there.
    - **Revisionist note:** James Fazio, using newly released notebooks, argues that at the 1831 conference Darby called the idea "an absurdity." The Margaret MacDonald (1830) influence theory is speculative.
    - URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nelson_Darby ; https://brethrenarchive.org/Features/Powerscourt
    - Confidence: VERIFIED that the movement originated in the 1830s. The exact year of Darby's rapture view is DISPUTED.

29. **Scofield Reference Bible.**
    - Oxford University Press, **1909**; revised 1917 and (New Scofield) 1967. More than **2 million** copies by c. 1943–1945. The lifetime "over 10 million" is an estimate, and no primary OUP sales data was found.
    - URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scofield_Reference_Bible ; https://textandcanon.org/the-bestselling-reference-bible-that-remade-american-evangelicalism
    - Confidence: VERIFIED (2M). PARTIAL (10M).

30. **Hal Lindsey, *The Late Great Planet Earth* (Zondervan, 1970).**
    - *The New York Times* named it the best-selling nonfiction book of the 1970s (NYT, Dec 30, 1979, per AFI).
    - Sales: about 10M by the end of the 1970s, **28M+ by 1990**, and an estimated 35M by 1999 (NEH *Humanities*).
    - URLs: https://www.neh.gov/humanities/2017/winter/feature/the-late-great-planet-earth-made-the-apocalypse-popular-concern ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Late_Great_Planet_Earth
    - Confidence: VERIFIED

31. **Left Behind series (LaHaye & Jenkins, Tyndale, 1995–2007).**
    - Tyndale figures over time: 9.5M (1999), 45M+ product line (2001), 63M+ (2011), **over 80M** "sixteen books and dozens of ancillary products" (PW, 2016, LaHaye obituary).
    - **80M includes ancillary products** (audio, kids' series and so on), not just the 16 novels. The original series ended with *Kingdom Come* (2007).
    - URLs: https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20011008/39773-no-end-for-end-times.html ; https://publishersweekly.com/lahaye
    - Confidence: VERIFIED (80M total product line). PARTIAL if quoted as "80M books of the novels."

## D. Tree 2 sources (relational gospel)

32. **Gustaf Aulén, *Christus Victor*.**
    - Delivered as lectures at Uppsala, March–April 1930. Swedish: *Den kristna försoningstanken* (1930). English translation by A. G. Hebert, SPCK, London, **1931**.
    - Aulén's typology: classic/dramatic, Latin (Anselmian) and subjective (Abelardian).
    - URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustaf_Aul%C3%A9n ; https://mail.biblicalstudies.org.uk/pdf/cjt/07-3_167.pdf
    - Confidence: VERIFIED

33. **Eastern Orthodox ancestral sin vs. inherited guilt.**
    - The Orthodox tradition speaks of "ancestral sin" (*propatorikon hamartema*): we inherit mortality and corruption, not Adam's guilt.
    - Key modern works: John Romanides, *To Propatorikon Hamartema* (doctoral thesis, Athens 1957); John Meyendorff, *Byzantine Theology* (1974), pp. 143–145 ("major issue of translation" on Rom 5:12).
    - URLs: https://www.neamericandiocese.org/menu/main-menu/original-sin/54 ; https://dehesa.unex.es/bitstream/10662/20970/1/2340-4256_18_525.pdf
    - Confidence: VERIFIED for the positions and dates. The Meyendorff quote is via secondary sources (PARTIAL).

34. **Partial preterism: Josephus, Eusebius, Sproul.**
    - Josephus, *Jewish War* (c. AD 75), is the main eyewitness account of AD 70 (found as standard knowledge; the passages were not re-fetched).
    - Eusebius, *Ecclesiastical History* III.5 (c. 313–325), records the flight of Jerusalem Christians to Pella "before the war," following a revelation. Its historicity is debated (Lüdemann says legend; Bourgel dates the flight to 68).
    - R. C. Sproul, *The Last Days According to Jesus* (Baker, 1998): "end of the age" means the end of the Jewish age, and the "generation" fits AD 30–70.
    - URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_to_Pella ; https://www.logos.com/resource/LLS$LSTDYSCCRDNGJSS
    - Confidence: VERIFIED (Sproul 1998, Eusebius III.5). The Pella flight's historicity is DISPUTED.

35. **Postmillennialism.**
    - Lineage runs from Daniel Whitby (1703) and Jonathan Edwards (*History of the Work of Redemption*, 1739 sermons, publ. 1774) to the Princeton theologians: Charles Hodge, A. A. Hodge and (debatably) B. B. Warfield.
    - It was dominant in 19th-century American Protestantism and declined around WWI and WWII. Wartime pessimism is the standard explanation; postmillennialists blame modernism.
    - URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmillennialism ; https://kimriddlebarger.com/princeton-and-the-millennium
    - Confidence: VERIFIED for the general history. Warfield's label and the cause of decline are DISPUTED.

## E. Modern grace, lordship and hell debates

36. **J. I. Packer, *Evangelism and the Sovereignty of God* (IVF/IVP, 1961).**
    - Grew out of a 1959 address. It defines antinomy as "an appearance of contradiction between conclusions which seem equally logical, reasonable or necessary" (p. 18), applied to divine sovereignty and human responsibility.
    - URLs: https://banneroftruth.org/uk/?p=2607 ; https://www.desiringgod.org/articles/a-response-to-j-i-packer-on-the-so-called-antinomy-between-the-sovereignty-of-god-and-human-responsibility

39. **John Stott and annihilationism.**

    - URLs: https://anglican.ink/2026/07/09/john-stott-annihilationism-and-the-evangelical-controversy/ ; https://christianitytoday.com/ct/2000/october23/undying-worm-unquenchable-fire.html
    - Confidence: VERIFIED

40. **Evangelical Alliance (UK) ACUTE report, *The Nature of Hell* (Paternoster, 2000, ed. David Hilborn).**

    - URLs: https://www.eauk.org/church/resources/theological-articles/upload/The-Nature-of-Hell-2.pdf ; https://www.eauk.org/church/resources/theological-articles/the-nature-of-hell.cfm
    - Confidence: VERIFIED
