## A. The finished work and the facets of the gospel

**1. Tetelestai ("It is finished", John 19:30) written on receipts as "paid in full"**
- Status: **DISPUTED. The popular claim is wrong.**
- Origin: Moulton & Milligan, *Vocabulary of the Greek Testament* (1914–29), p. 630. It says "receipts are often introduced by the phrase τετέλεσται, usually written in an abbreviated manner". M&M relied on Grenfell & Hunt's 1897 edition of Egyptian customs receipts (P.Grenf. 2.50a–c). That edition expanded the abbreviation τετελ( ) as τετέλ(εσται).
- Correction: Of about 315 Arsinoite customs receipts, 17 spell the word out in full. Every one of those 17 reads **τετελώνηται** ("customs paid", from τελωνέω). None reads τετέλεσται. Papyrologists have expanded the abbreviation this way since about 1934. No ancient source has been found that uses τετέλεσται to mean "paid in full". BDAG files John 19:30 under the senses "complete/fulfil", not under "pay".
- Better reading: Jesus has finished the work the Father gave him (John 4:34; 5:36; 17:4), and Scripture has been fulfilled (19:28 uses τελειωθῇ next to τετέλεσται).
- Sources: Gary Manning Jr (Talbot), "Paid in Full? The Meaning of τετέλεσται", Biola Good Book Blog, 20 Apr 2022, https://www.biola.edu/blogs/good-book-blog/2022/paid-in-full-the-meaning-of-tetelestai-in-jesus-final-words ; summary with papyri.info links: https://ready4eternity.com/is-paid-in-full-tetelestai-sermon-folklore/ (2026); http://papyri.info/ddbdp/p.grenf;2;50a
- Safe wording: "The perfect tense means 'it has been brought to completion and stands complete'. The 'receipt' story is a twentieth-century sermon illustration based on a misread abbreviation."

- Quote (ANF 1, Roberts-Rambaut): "when He became incarnate, and was made man, He commenced afresh the long line of human beings, and furnished us, in a brief, comprehensive manner, with salvation; so that what we had lost in Adam — namely, to be according to the image and likeness of God — that we might recover in Christ Jesus."
- Note: This English translation does **not** use the word "recapitulated" in III.18.1. "Commenced afresh" translates Latin *in seipso recapitulavit*. If you want the English word itself, use **III.18.7**: "God recapitulated in Himself the ancient formation of man, that He might kill sin, deprive death of its power, and vivify man."
- URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103318.htm. Status: **VERIFIED**

## B. "The Blessing" and covenant

**4. Hebrew frequencies**
- בָּרַךְ *barak* (verb, H1288): **330** occurrences (Strong's/BibleHub concordance); BDB gives **329**, of which 233 are Piel. Status: **VERIFIED**. https://biblehub.com/hebrew/1288.htm
- בְּרָכָה *berakah* (noun, H1293): **69** (BibleHub; the "about 71" sometimes quoted depends on edition and counting). Status: **VERIFIED** (69 by Strong's count). https://biblehub.com/hebrew/1293.htm
- חֶסֶד *hesed* (H2617): **247** (BDB and BibleHub). Two of these (Lev 20:17; Prov 14:34) are a homonym meaning "shame/reproach". That leaves about **245** for "steadfast love/kindness", more than half in the Psalms. Status: **VERIFIED**. https://biblehub.com/hebrew/2617.htm

**5. Ketef Hinnom silver amulets (KH1, KH2)**
- Found **1979** in Chamber 25, Cave 24, Ketef Hinnom, Jerusalem, during excavations directed by **Gabriel Barkay** (then of Tel Aviv University). Now in the Israel Museum.
- They contain a form of the priestly blessing (Num 6:24–26), and KH1 echoes Deut 7:9 / Dan 9:4 language. They are dated on script grounds to the late 7th or early 6th century BC (about 650–587; "c. 600 BC"). Barkay later preferred the early 6th century, before 586.
- Definitive re-edition: Barkay, Vaughn, Lundberg & Zuckerman, "The Amulets from Ketef Hinnom: A New Edition and Evaluation," *BASOR* 334 (2004): 41–71. The re-edition calls them "one of the most significant discoveries ever made" for biblical studies, and Kyle McCarter said it should "settle any controversy".
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketef_Hinnom_scrolls ; https://www.thetorah.com/article/the-ketef-hinnom-amulets-wearing-the-priestly-blessing-for-protection. Status: **VERIFIED**
- Caveats: they are protective amulets that quote a liturgical blessing, not a Bible manuscript. "Oldest surviving text paralleling Scripture" is more exact than "oldest copy of the Bible". They are about 400 years older than the Dead Sea Scrolls. A minority once proposed a later (Hellenistic) date, and the 2004 study answered it. Status: **PARTIAL** (exact wording of the minority view not fetched)
- Theological note: Num 6:27, "So shall they put my name upon the people of Israel, and I will bless them". The blessing is the placing of the Name (this links B and E).

**6. Ancient Near Eastern covenant forms**
- George E. Mendenhall, "Ancient Oriental and Biblical Law" and "Covenant Forms in Israelite Tradition," *Biblical Archaeologist* 17 (1954), nos. 2 and 3. Reprinted as *Law and Covenant in Israel and the Ancient Near East* (1955). He compared Sinai and Deuteronomy with Hittite suzerain treaties of the 14th–13th c. BC, building on V. Korošec (1931).
- Treaty elements: preamble; historical prologue; stipulations; deposit and public reading; divine witnesses; blessings and curses (with oath and sanctions).
- Meredith G. Kline, *Treaty of the Great King* (1963): Deuteronomy as a whole is a suzerainty-treaty document. Later: *The Structure of Biblical Authority* (1972).
- Status: **NOT VERIFIED** online (standard bibliography, high confidence).
- Gen 15:9–17: Abram cuts a heifer, she-goat and ram in two. The "smoking fire pot and flaming torch" (God alone) passes between the pieces. Jer 34:18–20 shows the same rite as a self-curse oath: "the calf which they cut in two and passed between its parts". Hebrew idiom *karat berit* means "to cut a covenant". Status: **VERIFIED** (biblical text)
- The standard five covenants: Noahic (Gen 9:8–17, sign the rainbow); Abrahamic (Gen 12:1–3; 15; 17, sign circumcision); Mosaic/Sinai (Exod 19–24, sign the Sabbath, Exod 31:13–17); Davidic (2 Sam 7:12–16; Ps 89); New (Jer 31:31–34; Ezek 36:26–27; Luke 22:20; Heb 8:8–12, the longest OT quotation in the NT). Status: **VERIFIED**

**7. Gal 3:13–14 commentary**
- Two ἵνα (purpose) clauses state two results of Christ "becoming a curse": (a) Abraham's blessing reaches the Gentiles "in Christ Jesus"; (b) "we" (Jews and Gentiles together) receive "the promise of the Spirit", meaning the promised Spirit, through faith.
- Meyer: the second clause "is parallel to the first sentence of purpose by way of climax". The Expositor's Greek Testament: "Two gracious purposes of the Redeemer are here coupled together." Vincent: the blessing's content is the life that comes through the Spirit, the opposite of the curse (death).
- Ellicott: "The promise is said to be 'received' by the generation on which it is fulfilled" (compare Acts 2:33).
- Source: https://biblehub.com/commentaries/galatians/3-14.htm (19th-century public-domain commentaries). Status: **VERIFIED**
- Takeaway: Paul treats "the Blessing of Abraham" and "the Spirit" as two sides of one gift (compare 3:2–5, 8–9). The blessing is not mainly material prosperity.

## C. "Manifestation of the sons of God" (Rom 8:19)

**8a. Greek vocabulary in Rom 8:19–23**
- ἀποκαραδοκία (8:19; also Phil 1:20 only): "eager expectation", literally craning the head forward. ἀποκάλυψις (8:19): "revealing/unveiling" (KJV "manifestation"). It is something that happens to the sons, not something they achieve. υἱοθεσία (8:15, 23; 9:4; Gal 4:5; Eph 1:5): "adoption as sons". In 8:23 it is still awaited and is defined as "the redemption of our bodies".
- Status: **VERIFIED** (from the text; standard lexica)

**8b. Roman adoption**
- *Adoptio* was the adoption of someone still under another father's power (*patria potestas*), done by threefold mancipation and cession. *Adrogatio* was the adoption of a person who was legally independent (*sui iuris*). It required a vote of the curiate assembly, and the adoptee's whole household and property passed to the adopter. Source: Gaius, *Institutes* 1.97–107; Justinian, *Institutes* 1.11.
- The adoptee took the adopter's name and became his heir with full rights. Old debts and ties to the former family lapsed.
- Example: Octavian was adopted posthumously in Julius Caesar's will (44 BC) and became "C. Julius Caesar". Testamentary adoption was legally unusual, and he had it confirmed by a *lex curiata* in 43 BC. Tiberius was adopted by Augustus in AD 4. Trajan adopted Hadrian.
- Status: **NOT VERIFIED** online (standard Roman law, high confidence). Avoid overstating: a Jewish OT background (Exod 4:22; 2 Sam 7:14; Rom 9:4) is also argued (e.g., J. M. Scott, *Adoption as Sons of God*, 1992).

**8c. υἱός vs τέκνον ("teknon = child, huios = mature son")**
- Status: **DISPUTED. Not supported for Paul.**
- In Rom 8 itself Paul alternates the words: υἱοί (8:14), τέκνα (8:16), τέκνα → heirs (8:17), υἱῶν (8:19), τέκνων (8:21, "the glorious freedom of the *children* of God"). The very freedom revealed at the "manifestation" belongs to the τέκνα. Commentators (Cranfield, Moo, Dunn) treat the words as interchangeable here.
- John is the real distinction: he keeps υἱός for Jesus and calls believers τέκνα (John 1:12; 1 John 3:1–2). Paul sometimes uses υἱός for the adult heir in contrast to the minor (νήπιος), not to τέκνον (Gal 4:1–7).
- So the "maturity" contrast is νήπιος vs υἱός/heir in Gal 4, not τέκνον vs υἱός.

**9a. Latter Rain / "Manifest Sons of God"**
- Revival at Sharon Orphanage and Schools, North Battleford, Saskatchewan, beginning **11–12 Feb 1948** (George and Ern Hawtin, Percy Hunt, Herrick Holt).
- Assemblies of God USA, 23rd General Council (**1949**), Resolution 7: "We disapprove of those extreme teachings and practices ... this 23rd General Council disapproves of the so-called 'New Order of the Latter Rain'." The AG explicitly rejected the Manifest Sons doctrine; see also the AG 2000 position paper "End-Time Revival".
- George Warnock, *The Feast of Tabernacles* (**1951**): the feasts "consummat[e] in 'the manifestation of the Sons of God' – the 'overcomers' who will step into immortality and establish the Kingdom of God on earth."
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Rain_(post%E2%80%93World_War_II_movement) (with Riss, *Pneuma* 4 (1982); Holdcroft, *Pneuma* 2 (1980)). Status: **VERIFIED** (secondary source)

**9b. Patristic theosis as the historic orthodox parallel**
- 2 Pet 1:4: "partakers of the divine nature" (θείας κοινωνοὶ φύσεως). Irenaeus V pref. (above); Athanasius *Inc.* 54.3 (above). Catholic Catechism §460 quotes 2 Pet 1:4, Irenaeus, Athanasius and Aquinas.
- Eastern Orthodox teaching (e.g., Maximus the Confessor, Gregory Palamas; V. Lossky, *Mystical Theology of the Eastern Church*, 1944/1957): union with God by grace, through God's energies, never his essence. The creature–Creator distinction always remains.
- Contrast with Manifest Sons teaching: orthodox theosis is the whole Church's participation in Christ, completed at the bodily resurrection (Rom 8:23; 1 John 3:2). It is not an elite group of "overcomers" reaching immortality before the Parousia.
- Status: **PARTIAL** (texts verified above; the Orthodox summary is not separately fetched)

**10e. Augustine, *City of God* XXII.8 (written c. 426)**
- Exact wording: "It is not yet two years since these relics were first brought to Hippo-regius, and though many of the miracles which have been wrought by it have not, as I have the most certain means of knowing, been recorded, those which have been published amount to **almost seventy** at the hour at which I write."

He also says: "For even now miracles are wrought in the name of Christ, whether by His sacraments or by the prayers or relics of His saints; but they are not so brilliant and conspicuous..."

- URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120122.htm (NPNF1 vol. 2, Dods). Status: **VERIFIED**

Augustine changed his view: compare *Retractations* I.13.7 (c. 427), where he revises his earlier claim (*De vera religione* 25.47) that miracles had ceased (not fetched).

**12. Number of healing accounts in the Gospels**
- Counts depend on method. Morton Kelsey, *Healing and Christianity* (1973; rev. 1995), counts **41 distinct accounts of physical and mental healing** (72 accounts including parallels). He says nearly one-fifth of the Gospels (727 of 3,779 verses) concerns healing.
- Other common counts: about 26–30 individual healings plus about 12 summary statements (e.g., Matt 4:23–24; 8:16; 9:35; 12:15; 14:14, 35–36; 15:30; Mark 1:32–34; 3:10; 6:56; Luke 4:40; 6:17–19; Acts 10:38).
- Status: **PARTIAL** (Kelsey figures are widely cited; I could not open the book itself)
- Safe wording: "roughly 25–30 individual healing stories (about 40 counting exorcisms and parallels), plus a dozen 'he healed them all' summaries."

**13. James 5:14–16 in the early church, and Basil's hospital**
- *Apostolic Tradition* ch. 5 (attributed to Hippolytus, about 215; dating and authorship disputed) has a prayer over offered oil: that it may give "strength to all who taste it and health to all who use it". Serapion's *Euchologion* (about 350) has a prayer for the oil of the sick. Pope Innocent I, *Letter to Decentius* (416), §8, applies James 5 to anointing by priests and by lay faithful.
- Status: **PARTIAL** (texts not fetched; standard references)
- Tertullian, *Scapula* 4 (above): Proculus cured Severus "by anointing". Status: **VERIFIED**
- Basil's *Basileias* (Caesarea, about 369–372): Gregory of Nazianzus, *Oration* 43 (funeral oration for Basil) §63: "Go forth a little way from the city, and behold the new city, the storehouse of piety ... where disease is regarded in a religious light, and disaster is thought a blessing, and sympathy is put to the test." URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310243.htm. Status: **VERIFIED**

## E. Proclaiming / calling on the name of the LORD

**14a. Occurrences of the Tetragrammaton and Jewish practice**
- YHWH occurs **6,828** times in BHK/BHS (Masoretic Text). The masorah notes 134 further places where scribes replaced it with Adonai. The Jewish Encyclopedia gave 5,410, a different counting basis.
- In reading, Jews say *Adonai* ("my Lord"), or *Elohim* when it follows Adonai. In everyday speech they say *HaShem* ("the Name").
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetragrammaton. Status: **VERIFIED** (6,828 vs 6,823 depends on edition; say "about 6,800")

**14b. Key texts** (all VERIFIED from the biblical text)
- Exod 3:14–15: "I AM WHO I AM" (*ehyeh asher ehyeh*). "This is my name forever, and thus I am to be remembered (*zikri*) throughout all generations."
- Exod 33:19: "I will make all my goodness pass before you and will proclaim before you my name 'The LORD'" (*weqarati beshem YHWH lefaneka*).
- Exod 34:5–7: "he stood with him there and proclaimed the name of the LORD" (*wayyiqra beshem YHWH*). Then comes the content of the name: "The LORD, the LORD, a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love (*hesed*) and faithfulness..." The Name is defined by character, not titles. This passage is echoed about 8–9 times later (Num 14:18; Neh 9:17; Ps 86:15; 103:8; 145:8; Joel 2:13; Jonah 4:2; Nah 1:3).
- The same Hebrew phrase *qara beshem YHWH* means both "call on" (in worship) and "proclaim" the name: Gen 4:26 ("people began to call upon the name of the LORD"); 12:8; 13:4; 21:33; 26:25. Abraham builds an altar and "called on the name". Status: **VERIFIED** (grammatical point standard; the ambiguity is discussed in commentaries)
- Deut 12:5, 11: "the place that the LORD your God will choose ... to put his name and make his habitation there" / "to make his name dwell there" (*leshakken shemo sham*). This is "Name theology". See also 1 Kgs 8:29.
- Num 6:27: "So shall they put my name upon the people of Israel, and I will bless them."

**15a. Meaning of *shem***
- BDB s.v. שֵׁם: "name", including "reputation, fame, glory" and "the name of Yahweh" as his revealed character and presence. TWOT (#2405) and *TDOT* XV (s.v. *šēm*) treat the divine Name as a stand-in for God's self-revealed presence and authority (Deut 12; 1 Kgs 8; Ps 20:1; Prov 18:10, "The name of the LORD is a strong tower").
- Status: **NOT VERIFIED** online (standard lexica, high confidence)
- Ellicott on Matt 28:19 (fetched): "the Name of God is a revelation of what He is" (citing Exod 3:14–15). Status: **VERIFIED**

**15b. John 17:6, 26**
- 17:6: "I have manifested (ἐφανέρωσα) your name to the people whom you gave me." 17:11–12: "keep them in your name, which you have given me". 17:26: "I made known to them your name, and I will continue to make it known, that the love with which you have loved me may be in them, and I in them."
- Revealing the Name means revealing the Father's character and love, and it produces indwelling. Status: **VERIFIED** (text)

**15c. "In the name of Jesus" in Acts**
- ὄνομα with reference to Jesus occurs about 33 times in Acts. Key places: 2:21, 38; 3:6, 16; 4:7, 10, 12, 17–18, 30; 5:28, 40–41; 8:12, 16; 9:14–16, 21, 27–28; 10:43, 48; 15:26; 16:18; 19:5, 13, 17; 21:13; 22:16; 26:9.
- Status: **PARTIAL** (list compiled from the text; exact totals vary between about 30 and 35 by counting method)
- Healing link: Acts 3:6, "In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, rise up and walk", and 3:16, "his name — by faith in his name — has made this man strong."

**15d. Phil 2:9–11, Isa 45:23, Joel 2:32 / Rom 10:13**
- Isa 45:22–23 (LXX): "to me every knee shall bow, every tongue shall swear/confess", said by YHWH, the only God, in a strongly monotheistic passage. Phil 2:10–11 applies it to Jesus, who receives "the name above every name", meaning *Kyrios*, the LXX substitute for YHWH.
- Joel 2:32 (3:5 MT) "everyone who calls on the name of the LORD (YHWH) shall be saved" is applied to Jesus in Rom 10:9–13 and Acts 2:21, 36. 1 Cor 1:2 defines Christians as those "who call upon the name of our Lord Jesus Christ". This continues the Gen 4:26 / 12:8 practice of "calling on the Name".
- Richard Bauckham, *Jesus and the God of Israel: God Crucified and Other Studies on the New Testament's Christology of Divine Identity* (Eerdmans, **2008**), which expands *God Crucified* (1998). Phil 2:6–11 is a key text for "divine identity Christology".
- Larry Hurtado, *One God, One Lord* (1988) and *Lord Jesus Christ: Devotion to Jesus in Earliest Christianity* (Eerdmans, 2003): early "binitarian" devotion, including calling on Jesus' name in worship.
- Status: **NOT VERIFIED** online (bibliographic details high confidence)

**16. Matt 28:19, "into the name" (singular)**
- The Greek is εἰς τὸ ὄνομα, "*into* the name" (not "in"). The singular is "name", not "names", of Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
- Ellicott: "the name (not 'the names') of God". Expositor's Greek Testament: "it is the name not of one but of three... Hence might be deduced the idea of a Trinity constituting at the same time a Divine Unity. But this would probably be reading more into the words than was intended." Meyer warns against making dogmatic capital of the singular. He notes "in nomine" (Vulgate) is a mistranslation of *in nomen*, and that Tertullian (*De bapt.* 13) has *in nomen*. Vincent: "into" means union or communion with, and "the name ... is the expression of the sum total of the divine Being."
- Source: https://biblehub.com/commentaries/matthew/28-19.htm. Status: **VERIFIED**
- Use: baptism places a person into the Name (compare Num 6:27, "put my name upon them"). Commentators disagree on how much Trinitarian theology the singular proves.

## Commonly repeated but wrong or overstated

1. **Tetelestai = "paid in full" on receipts.** This is false. It comes from a 1897 misexpansion of τετελ( ) that is really τετελώνηται, "customs paid" (Manning 2022).
2. **"Recapitulated" in Irenaeus III.18.1.** The public-domain English there reads "commenced afresh". The word "recapitulated" appears in III.18.7.
3. **Calvin's "as long as Christ remains outside of us"** is the 1960 Battles translation, still under copyright. The public-domain Beveridge text reads differently.
4. **Augustine's "seventy healings".** He wrote "almost seventy" *published accounts* in under two years at Hippo. They were relic-related, and he says many more went unrecorded.
5. **teknon = infant, huios = mature son.** Paul uses the words interchangeably in Rom 8:14–21, and 8:21 puts the freedom of the manifestation with the τέκνα.
6. **Ketef Hinnom as "the oldest Bible manuscript".** It is better described as the oldest surviving text paralleling Scripture: amulets quoting the priestly blessing, c. 600 BC.
7. **Origen's "traces".** Origen says only "traces" of the Spirit's gifts remained, a weaker claim than "healings were common".
8. **berakah "71".** Strong's/BibleHub count 69. The hesed count of 247 includes 2 homonyms meaning "shame".
