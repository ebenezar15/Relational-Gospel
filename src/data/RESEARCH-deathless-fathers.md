# The Church Fathers on immortality, incorruption and death: verified quotations

Method: each quotation below was checked word for word against the full page text fetched on 2026-10-23 (newadvent.org and ccel.org through Apify web-fetch). Unless noted, the translations are the public-domain Ante-Nicene Fathers (ANF, 1885) and Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers (NPNF, 1886-1900) versions as edited on New Advent. Brackets [ ] belong to the translators. An ellipsis (...) marks an omission I made. Dates are approximate.

Status key:
- VERIFIED: the exact wording was confirmed on the cited page.
- PARTIAL: the wording was confirmed, but the translation is not public domain or the attribution is uncertain.
- NOT VERIFIED: the wording could not be confirmed.

---

1. **Ignatius of Antioch, *Epistle to the Ephesians* ch. 20 (modern numbering 20.2), c. 107-110**
   - Quote: "breaking one and the same bread, which is the medicine of immortality, and the antidote to prevent us from dying, but [which causes] that we should live for ever in Jesus Christ."
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0104.htm
   - Meaning: Ignatius was on his way to martyrdom and expected to die (ch. 1, 12). "Not dying" here means eternal life in Christ given through the Eucharist, not escape from physical death. (Also see ch. 19: "He meditated the abolition of death"; ch. 17: "that He might breathe immortality into His Church.")
   - Status: VERIFIED

2. **Theophilus of Antioch, *To Autolycus* II.27, c. 180**
   - Quote: "He was by nature neither mortal nor immortal. For if He had made him immortal from the beginning, He would have made him God. Again, if He had made him mortal, God would seem to be the cause of his death."
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02042.htm
   - Meaning: Adam was created "capable of both". Immortality was to be received "as reward" for obedience, and believers now obtain it as a gift and "obtaining the resurrection, can inherit incorruption" (same chapter). The "now" is a gift received by faith. The bodily fulfilment comes at the resurrection.
   - Status: VERIFIED

3. **Theophilus of Antioch, *To Autolycus* II.26, c. 180** (added; this is the source of the "remoulded vessel" image)
   - Quote: "For just as a vessel, when on being fashioned it has some flaw, is remoulded or remade, that it may become new and entire; so also it happens to man by death. For somehow or other he is broken up, that he may rise in the resurrection whole"
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/02042.htm
   - Meaning: Death is treated as a merciful remaking. God "did not allow him to remain in sin for ever", and the believer's body is broken so it can be restored at the resurrection.
   - Status: VERIFIED

4. **Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* III.19.1, c. 180**
   - Quote: "For by no other means could we have attained to incorruptibility and immortality, unless we had been united to incorruptibility and immortality."
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103319.htm
   - Meaning: The Word became man so that our humanity could be joined to his incorruptible life "so that the corruptible might be swallowed up by incorruptibility, and the mortal by immortality". This is the foundation of patristic "deification" language. In III.19.3 the actual rising of "the remaining part of the body" happens "when the time is fulfilled".
   - Status: VERIFIED

5. **Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* III.23.6, c. 180**
   - Quote: "not because He envied him the tree of life ... but because He pitied him, [and did not desire] that he should continue a sinner for ever, nor that the sin which surrounded him should be immortal, and evil interminable and irremediable."
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103323.htm
   - Meaning: God set death as a boundary to sin "by the dissolution of the flesh", so that man "dying to it, might begin to live to God". Physical death is a mercy, not only a defeat. III.23.7 adds: "his salvation is death's destruction".
   - Status: VERIFIED

6. **Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* IV.38.3, c. 180**
   - Quote: "being in subjection to God is continuance in immortality, and immortality is the glory of the uncreated One ... the beholding of God is productive of immortality, but immortality renders one near unto God."
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103438.htm
   - Meaning: Humanity was created "infantile" and must grow toward the uncreated God. Immortality comes through a process of growth and the vision of God. IV.38.4 then quotes "But you shall die like men" as an admission of human weakness.
   - Status: VERIFIED

7. **Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* V.2.3, c. 180**
   - Quote: "so also our bodies, being nourished by it, and deposited in the earth, and suffering decomposition there, shall rise at their appointed time, the Word of God granting them resurrection"
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103502.htm
   - Meaning: This is the clearest early statement that Eucharistic "medicine of immortality" does not prevent burial and decay. The flesh is made capable of incorruption and rises later. Irenaeus also suggests God "permitted our resolution into the common dust" to teach us humility.
   - Status: VERIFIED

8. **Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* V.5.1, c. 180 (Enoch and Elijah)**
   - Quote: "For Enoch, when he pleased God, was translated in the same body in which he did please Him, thus pointing out by anticipation the translation of the just."
   - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103505.htm
   - Meaning: The translated bodies of Enoch and Elijah are offered as proof that flesh can receive eternal life (the chapter heading says "God can raise up our bodies to life eternal"). Irenaeus records the elders' teaching that the translated "remain until the consummation [of all things], as a prelude to immortality". He treats them as signs of the future resurrection, not as a model for believers today.
   - Status: VERIFIED

9. **Melito of Sardis, *On Pascha (Peri Pascha)* §102 (with §§100-103), c. 160-180**
   - Quote: "I am the one who destroyed death, and triumphed over the enemy, and trampled Hades under foot, and bound the strong one, and carried off man to the heights of heaven, I, he says, am the Christ."
   - URL: https://www.kerux.com/doc/0401A1.asp (Kerux: Journal of the Northwest Theological Seminary 4/1, May 1989)
   - Meaning: In this Easter proclamation the risen Christ himself speaks, and the victory over death is his. In §103 the promise to believers is future ("I am your resurrection ... I will raise you up by my right hand"). §55 calls bodily death the separation of the body from the soul.
   - Status: PARTIAL. The wording and section number were confirmed, but no public-domain English translation exists. The text was first published in 1940, and this Kerux translation is under copyright, so cite it as a short quotation. Translations differ (for example, "crushed Hades").

10. **Tatian, *Address to the Greeks* ch. 13, c. 165-172**
    - Quote: "The soul is not in itself immortal, O Greeks, but mortal. Yet it is possible for it not to die. ... if it acquires the knowledge of God, it dies not, although for a time it be dissolved."
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0202.htm
    - Meaning: Immortality is not built into human nature. It is a gift that comes through union with the Spirit. Even for the one who "dies not", there is still a temporary "dissolution", so "not dying" means not perishing, while physical death still happens. (Tatian later became heterodox, and Irenaeus attacks him in III.23.8.)
    - Status: VERIFIED

11. **Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* §21.1-2, c. 318-335 (key text on why Christians still die)**
    - Quote: "we, the faithful in Christ, no longer die the death as before ... henceforth we are only dissolved, agreeably to our bodies' mortal nature, at the time God has fixed for each, that we may be able to gain a better resurrection."
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2802.htm
    - Meaning: The condemnation and corruption of death are gone, but believers still undergo bodily "dissolution" at a time God has fixed. Athanasius continues: "like the seeds which are cast into the earth, we do not perish by dissolution, but sown in the earth, shall rise again."
    - Status: VERIFIED. The NPNF translation is by Archibald Robertson.

12. **Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* §9.1-2, c. 318-335**
    - Quote: "He takes to Himself a body capable of death, that it ... might be worthy to die in the stead of all, and might ... remain incorruptible, and that thenceforth corruption might be stayed from all by the Grace of the Resurrection."
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2802.htm
    - Meaning: The Word clothed all "with incorruption, by the promise of the resurrection". Corruption is stopped through the resurrection, which is a promise. (§20 adds: "none other could render the mortal immortal, save our Lord Jesus Christ".)
    - Status: VERIFIED

13. **Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* §27.1-2, c. 318-335**
    - Quote: "now that the Saviour has raised His body, death is no longer terrible; for all who believe in Christ tread him under as nought, and choose rather to die than to deny their faith in Christ."
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2802.htm
    - Meaning: Athanasius's proof that death is defeated is that Christians no longer fear it, and the example he gives is martyrs willingly dying. They "know that when they die they are not destroyed, but actually [begin to] live, and become incorruptible through the Resurrection." Victory over death means courage in dying, not avoiding death.
    - Status: VERIFIED

14. **Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* §§27.4 (Robertson's paragraph 4 in "chapter" 27) and 29.4-5, c. 318-335**
    - Quote (§27): "all they who are in Christ, as they pass by, trample on him, and witnessing to Christ scoff at death, jesting at him, and saying ... O death, where is your victory? O grave, where is your sting."
    - Quote (§29): "For who that sees a lion, either, made sport of by children, fails to see that he is either dead or has lost all his power?"
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2802.htm
    - Meaning: Death is like a defeated tyrant bound and mocked. The evidence is "men and females and young children rushing and leaping upon death for the sake of Christ's religion" (§29.4), which again refers to martyrdom. Note that the "trampling" passage is in New Advent's §27, not §28.
    - Status: VERIFIED. Robertson's paragraph numbering inside each chapter is irregular on New Advent, so cite by chapter.

15. **Athanasius, *On the Incarnation* §47 (end), c. 318-335**
    - Quote: "Christ alone ... has throughout all the world persuaded whole churches full of men to despise death, and to mind the things of immortality"
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2802.htm
    - Meaning: Contempt for death and a focus on immortality are presented as the moral fruit of the gospel, in contrast with the Greek philosophers' failure to persuade people.
    - Status: VERIFIED

16. **Methodius of Olympus, *From the Discourse on the Resurrection* Part I, §IV, c. 300**
    - Quote: "In order, then, that man might not be an undying or ever-living evil ... God for this cause pronounced him mortal, and clothed him with mortality. For this is what was meant by the coats of skins"
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0625.htm
    - Meaning: Physical death is a mercy that uproots sin "by the dissolution of the body". §VI compares the body to a statue that has been defaced and is melted down and recast: "the melting down of the statue ... corresponds to the death and dissolution of the body ... the remoulding ... to the resurrection after death". Part I §X says death was appointed "that evil might not remain forever", and adds that "it has been made by others of our Fathers as well".
    - Status: VERIFIED

17. **Cyril of Jerusalem, *Catechetical Lecture* 18 §18, c. 350**
    - Quote: "For this body shall be raised not remaining weak as now; but raised the very same body, though by putting on incorruption it shall be fashioned anew — as iron blending with fire becomes fire"
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310118.htm
    - Meaning: The body will be raised "an eternal body ... made spiritual". Incorruption is put on at the resurrection. The whole lecture (§§1-21) defends the future bodily resurrection of people who have actually died, and §1 calls "the hope of the Resurrection" "the root of all good works".
    - Status: VERIFIED

18. **Gregory of Nazianzus, *Oration* 45 (Second Easter Oration) §8, c. 383-390**
    - Quote: "Yet here too he makes a gain, namely death and the cutting off of sin, in order that evil may not be immortal. Thus, his punishment is changed into a mercy"
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310245.htm
    - Meaning: This is the same tradition found in Irenaeus III.23.6 and Methodius. Death keeps evil from becoming immortal. (Nearly the same words appear in Oration 38.12, which was not separately checked.)
    - Status: VERIFIED

19. **Gregory of Nyssa, *Great Catechism (Catechetical Oration)* ch. 8, c. 385**
    - Quote: "the maker of our vessel, now that wickedness has intermingled with our sentient part ... will dissolve the material which has received the evil, and, re-moulding it again by the Resurrection without any admixture of the contrary matter, will recombine the elements"
    - URL: https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/npnf205.xi.ii.x.html
    - Meaning: Mortality is the "coats of skins", a liability to death that is "not ... to last for ever" and "laid no hold upon the Divine image". Death is a purifying dissolution, like a potter breaking a clay pot that has been filled with lead, and the resurrection reshapes it. Gregory also says that "Man, like some earthen potsherd, is resolved again into the dust ... that he may, through the resurrection, be reformed anew." (The New Advent page 2908.htm only has the synopsis, so use the CCEL page.)
    - Status: VERIFIED. The NPNF translation is by William Moore and Henry Austin Wilson.

20. **John Chrysostom (attributed), *Paschal (Catechetical) Homily*, late 4th or early 5th century**
    - Quote: "Let no one fear death, for the Saviour's death has set us free. He that was taken by death has annihilated it! ... Christ is risen, and life reigns! Christ is risen, and not one dead remains in a tomb!"
    - URL: https://en.orthodoxwiki.org/Paschal_Homily
    - Meaning: This is a liturgical proclamation of Christ's victory that ends: "For Christ, being raised from the dead, has become the First-fruits of them that slept." "Not one dead remains in a tomb" is a proclamation of what Easter guarantees, not a literal claim that the graves are now empty.
    - Status: PARTIAL. The wording matches the OrthodoxWiki translation, but the authorship is traditional and not certain. The homily is not in NPNF, and the copyright status of the English translation is unclear. Translations vary: the requested wording "death is annihilated" does not match this one, which reads "Christ is risen, and you are overthrown!"

21. **Augustine, *City of God* XIII.4, c. 417-418 (the most direct answer to "why do the regenerate still die?")**
    - Quote: "the parting of soul and body was left, though its connection with sin was removed, for this reason, that if the immortality of the body followed immediately upon the sacrament of regeneration, faith itself would be thereby enervated."
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120113.htm
    - Meaning: Augustine directly rejects the idea that the baptized should stop dying physically. Bodily death remains so that faith can stay faith, hoping for what is unseen, and so that martyrdom is possible. "Now it is said to the martyr, Die, that you sin not." (He points to his earlier treatise *On the Merits and Remission of Sins*, which he calls his work "on the baptism of infants".)
    - Status: VERIFIED. The NPNF translation is by Marcus Dods.

22. **Augustine, *City of God* XIII.2 and XIII.8, c. 417-418 (first and second death)**
    - Quote (XIII.8): "He submits to the separation of soul and body, lest the soul be separated both from God and from the body, and so the whole first death be completed, and the second death receive him everlastingly."
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/120113.htm
    - Meaning: Augustine distinguishes the first death (the soul leaving the body), the death of the soul (God leaving it) and the second death (eternal damnation). Believers undergo the first in order to escape the second. XIII.2: "The death, then, of the soul takes place when God forsakes it, as the death of the body when the soul forsakes it."
    - Status: VERIFIED

23. **Augustine, *On Rebuke and Grace* ch. 12, §33, c. 426-427 (posse non mori / non posse mori)**
    - Quote: "the first immortality was to be able not to die, the last will be much greater, not to be able to die"
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1513.htm
    - Meaning: Adam had the ability not to die (*posse non mori*). The saints in glory will have the inability to die (*non posse mori*). In between, fallen and redeemed humanity is still mortal. The fuller immortality is located in the final state. New Advent heads this section "Chapter 33 [XII.]".
    - Status: VERIFIED

24. **Augustine, *Tractates on John* 43 §§10-11 (on John 8:51), c. 414-420**
    - Quote: "what mean His words, He who keeps my saying shall never see death; save that the Lord saw another death ... the second death, death eternal ... This is real death; for that other is only a removal."
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1701043.htm
    - Meaning: "Never see death" refers to the second death, not bodily death. §12 adds: "As a man, you are born — art destined to die ... Die then you must; you have no means of escape."
    - Status: VERIFIED. The NPNF translation is by John Gibb.

25. **Augustine, *Tractates on John* 49 §15 (on John 11:25-26), c. 414-420**
    - Quote: "whosoever lives in the flesh, and believes in me, though he shall die in time on account of the death of the flesh, shall never die, because of the life of the spirit, and the immortality of the resurrection."
    - URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/1701049.htm
    - Meaning: Augustine reads "shall never die" as a present life of the spirit plus the future resurrection, "till the flesh also rise again, never more to die". §2 adds that the death of the flesh "must certainly come some time", and §10 says the saints at rest still await "the resurrection of the flesh, the destruction of death".
    - Status: VERIFIED

- Gregory of Nazianzus, *Oration* 38.12: parallel wording to Or. 45.8, not separately fetched.

## What the fathers collectively taught (for the study page)
1. Christ's death and resurrection really defeated death. They use strong victory language: "destroyed", "trampled", "annihilated", "abolished".
2. Immortality is a gift from God, not a natural possession (Theophilus, Tatian, Irenaeus). It begins now through union with Christ, the Spirit and the Eucharist (Ignatius, Irenaeus), but it is completed in the bodily resurrection.
3. Every father checked still expected believers to die physically. Athanasius §21 says believers are "only dissolved ... at the time God has fixed for each". Irenaeus V.2.3 has Eucharist-fed bodies "deposited in the earth" and rising later. Augustine XIII.4 says immortality right after baptism would destroy faith.
4. What changes is how death works for the believer. It is no longer condemnation but sleep, a sowing, or a recasting of a vessel or statue. Christians show the victory by no longer fearing death, which in practice meant martyrdom.
5. Death was even described as a mercy that stops evil from being immortal (Irenaeus, Theophilus, Methodius, Gregory of Nazianzus, Gregory of Nyssa).
6. Enoch and Elijah are the only people treated as having not died (Irenaeus V.5). They are a prophecy and pledge of the resurrection, and their state is "a prelude to immortality", not a pattern for believers to follow now.
7. "Shall never die" (John 8:51; 11:26) was read as escape from the second, eternal death. Bodily death and resurrection remain (Augustine, Tractates 43 and 49).
