## 1. Lexical: pneumatikos, barak/berakah, shalom

1.1 **"Spiritual" (pneumatikos) in Paul means "of or from the (Holy) Spirit", not "immaterial".**
- Figure: BDAG s.v. pneumatikos gives the main sense as "pertaining to the (divine) spirit", and under it "caused by or filled with the (divine) spirit". This is quoted from memory. I could not reach the licensed text online.
- Source: BDAG, 3rd ed. (Univ. of Chicago Press, 2000), p. 837.
- Confidence: NOT VERIFIED online. Check a print or Logos copy before quoting.

1.2 **N.T. Wright on 1 Cor 15:44 (soma psychikon / soma pneumatikon).**
- Figure: Wright argues that adjectives ending in -ikos describe what *animates* a thing, not what it is made of. So a psychikon body is "animated by the soul" (ordinary life), and a pneumatikon body is "animated by the Spirit". It is not a body made of "spirit". The discussion is at about pp. 347–356.
- Source: N.T. Wright, *The Resurrection of the Son of God* (Fortress, 2003). Seen only through secondary summaries.
- URL: https://catholicbibletalk.com/?p=156 (secondary); https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/review/the-resurrection-of-the-son-of-god/
- Confidence: PARTIAL. The substance is well known, but quote the exact wording from the book.

1.3 **Gordon Fee, *God's Empowering Presence* (Hendrickson, 1994; Baker reprint 2009).**
- Figure: Fee argues that Paul's pneumatikos almost always refers to the Holy Spirit ("Spirit-ual"). He calls Eph 1:3 "perhaps the most ambiguous" use, but still reads it as "blessings that pertain to the Spirit".
- URL: https://bakerpublishinggroup.com/books/god-s-empowering-presence/333230
- Confidence: PARTIAL. The "most ambiguous" wording was seen only in a search snippet of biblia.com p. 32, which I could not open.

1.4 **Context support from Ephesians itself.**
- Figure: Eph 6:12 uses ta pneumatika for "spiritual forces of evil". That shows the word means "belonging to the spirit realm or Spirit", not "nice" or "holy" or "non-physical good".
- Source: the text.
- Confidence: VERIFIED (text).

1.5 **Hebrew barak (Strong's H1288) and berakah (H1293).**
- barak: basic sense "kneel", hence "bless".
- berakah: BDB glosses it as "blessing", and also "prosperity" (Prov 11:11), "gift" (Gen 33:11) and "treaty of peace". Strong's: "benediction; by implication prosperity".
- So OT blessing is explicitly material as well as relational.
- URL: https://biblehub.com/hebrew/1293.htm ; https://www.bibletools.org/index.cfm/fuseaction/Lexicon.show/ID/H1288/barak.htm
- Confidence: VERIFIED (BDB/Strong's). The often-quoted TWOT gloss "endue with power for success, prosperity, fecundity" was NOT VERIFIED.

1.6 **Shalom.**
- Figure: BDB gives "completeness, soundness, welfare, peace". The word covers bodily health (Ps 38:3), prosperity, and right relationship.
- Confidence: PARTIAL (standard lexicon sense; no online page was fetched).

## 2. Gnosticism and Platonism basics

2.1 **Nag Hammadi library.**
- Figure: found in December 1945 near Nag Hammadi, Upper Egypt, by Muhammad Ali al-Samman and his brothers.
- Contents: 13 leather-bound papyrus codices with 52 tractates. Some are duplicates, giving about 46 distinct works. The collection includes the only complete Coptic Gospel of Thomas and a fragment of Plato's *Republic*. The codices are 4th-century Coptic translations of 2nd–3rd-century Greek originals, now in the Coptic Museum, Cairo.
- URL: https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/magazine/article/the-secret-is-out
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

2.2 **Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* (Adversus Haereses), c. 180.**
- Figure: Book 5.2 attacks those who "disallow the salvation of the flesh". The argument runs: if the flesh is not saved, then the Eucharist (created bread and wine) and Christ's real blood are emptied of meaning. Irenaeus defends the goodness of the Creator and of the creation against the Valentinians.
- URL: https://bkv.unifr.ch/en/works/cpg-1306/versions/against-heresies/divisions/790 ; https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103502.htm
- Confidence: VERIFIED. The date of c. 180 is the standard one (written under Eleutherus, c. 174–189).

2.3 **Marcion.**
- Figure: broke with, or was expelled from, the Roman church in about 144. He taught that the Creator God of the OT was a lesser god, distinct from the Father of Jesus. His canon was an edited Luke plus 10 Pauline letters. Tertullian's *Adversus Marcionem* (c. 207–208) is the main source.
- URL: https://tyndalebulletin.org/article/30463.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcion_of_Sinope
- Confidence: VERIFIED. The date 144 comes from Tertullian's reckoning, so treat it as "c. 144".

2.4 **Docetism.**
- Figure: the view that Christ only "seemed" (Greek dokein) to have flesh or to suffer. Ignatius, *To the Smyrnaeans* 2 (c. 110), answers those who say "he suffered only in semblance". 1 John 4:2–3 and 2 John 7 are earlier anti-docetic texts.
- URL: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0109.htm
- Confidence: VERIFIED (text; standard).

2.5 **Anti-ascetic New Testament texts.**
- 1 Tim 4:1–5: "forbid marriage and require abstinence from foods... everything created by God is good, and nothing is to be rejected if it is received with thanksgiving".
- Col 2:20–23: "Do not handle, Do not taste, Do not touch... have an appearance of wisdom in promoting self-made religion and asceticism and severity to the body, but they are of no value in stopping the indulgence of the flesh" (ESV).
- Confidence: VERIFIED (text). Calling these texts "anti-Gnostic" is an anachronism; they are better described as "anti-ascetic or proto-Gnostic".

2.6 **Plato, body as prison.**
- *Phaedo* 62b: humans are "in a kind of prison/guard-post" (phroura).
- *Phaedo* 82e: the soul is "fastened and glued to the body" and sees reality "through the bars of a prison".
- The "body = tomb" (soma/sema) pun is at *Cratylus* 400c, where Plato credits it to the Orphics. The pun is not in the Phaedo.
- URL: https://homepages.uc.edu/~martinj/Philosophy%20and%20Religion/Greek%20Background/Plato%20on%20the%20Soul.pdf
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the Phaedo. Cratylus 400c is PARTIAL (standard reference, not fetched).

2.7 **Debate over the category "Gnosticism".**
- Michael A. Williams, *Rethinking "Gnosticism": An Argument for Dismantling a Dubious Category* (Princeton UP, 1996; xix + 335 pp.). Williams argues that the label has no ancient self-designation behind it and that the stereotypes (anti-cosmic hatred of the body, either ascetic or libertine) don't fit the sources. He proposes the term "biblical demiurgical" traditions instead.
- Karen L. King, *What Is Gnosticism?* (Harvard UP, 2003). King argues that "Gnosticism" is a modern reification inherited from ancient orthodoxy-versus-heresy polemic.
- Counterpoint: David Brakke, *The Gnostics* (2010), keeps a narrower "Gnostic school" category.
- URL: https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691005423/rethinking-gnosticism ; https://bmcr.brynmawr.edu/?p=23367
- Confidence: VERIFIED. The "biblical demiurgical" term is Williams's proposal, but a search snippet did not show it; I rely on standard knowledge here.

## 3. "Neo-Gnosticism" critiques of modern evangelicalism

3.1 **N.T. Wright, *Surprised by Hope*.**
- Dates: SPCK (UK) 2007; HarperOne (US) 2008.
- Thesis: popular Christian hope has been distorted by Platonism, which pictures "going to heaven when you die" as the goal. The NT hope is bodily resurrection and new creation, with heaven and earth joined. Wright's well-known phrase "life after life after death" appears in the book.
- URL: https://www.bethinking.org/human-life/surprised-by-hope-by-tom-wright-a-review ; https://www.9marks.org/review/surprised-hope-nt-wright/
- Confidence: VERIFIED.
- The popular quote "heaven is important, but it's not the end of the world" comes from Wright's *Time* interview (Feb 2008). I did not confirm it in the book. Confidence for that quote: PARTIAL.

3.2 **Philip J. Lee, *Against the Protestant Gnostics* (Oxford UP, 1987; xx + 347 pp.).**
- Thesis: Gnostic motifs (elitist knowledge, escape from the world, individualism, contempt for the material world) are found inside American Protestantism, both liberal and conservative. Lee's remedies are more liturgy, discipline, preaching, and a church-centred faith. Reviewed by Robert M. Grant in *Church History* (1988).
- URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/church-history/article/against-the-protestant-gnostics-by-philip-j-lee-new-york-oxford-university-press-1987-xx-347-pp-2795/DA9DD5C7F02C6E62615E51709ED6D954
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

3.3 **Harold Bloom, *The American Religion: The Emergence of the Post-Christian Nation* (Simon & Schuster, 1992).**
- Thesis: American religion "masks itself as Protestant Christianity" but is really Gnostic: the inner self is uncreated and "alone with God". Mormons and Southern Baptists are its paradigms (E.Y. Mullins's "soul competency").
- URL: https://kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/harold-bloom/the-american-religion ; https://www.csmonitor.com/1992/0625/25152.html
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the thesis. The exact phrase "irretrievably Gnostic" is NOT VERIFIED; quote cautiously.
- Note: Bloom was a self-described "Jewish Gnostic" who partly admired this, and his reading is widely seen as idiosyncratic.

3.4 **J. Richard Middleton, *A New Heaven and a New Earth: Reclaiming Biblical Eschatology* (Baker Academic, 2014; 332 pp.).**
- Claim: "there is not one single reference in the entire biblical canon... to heaven as the eternal destiny of the believer". Middleton traces the otherworldly hope to Plato. The book won the 2014 Word Guild award.
- URL: https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/review/a-new-heaven-and-a-new-earth-reclaiming-biblical-eschatology/ ; https://muse.jhu.edu/article/646190/pdf
- Confidence: VERIFIED. The "no single reference" claim is contested by critics, who point to 2 Cor 5:1–8, Phil 1:23 and the intermediate state.

3.5 **Dispensationalism and the rapture.**
- J.N. Darby (1800–1882) developed pre-tribulation rapture teaching in about 1827–1833. It was aired at the Powerscourt conferences (1831–33) and spread through the Brethren. The Scofield Reference Bible (1909) popularised it in the US.
- The claim that Darby took the idea from Margaret MacDonald's 1830 vision (Dave MacPherson) is DISPUTED. Most historians find it unproven.
- Pew 2006: more than 80% of Pentecostals in every surveyed country believe in "the rapture of the Church".
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_MacDonald_(visionary) ; https://www.andrews.edu/library/car/cardigital/Periodicals/AUSS/1975-1/1975-1-15.pdf ; Pew: https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2006/10/05/spirit-and-power/
- Confidence: VERIFIED for Darby's role. The Pew rapture figure is VERIFIED from the executive summary.

3.6 **Hymn "This World Is Not My Home".**
- Figure: the words are traditional or anonymous. An early printed text is in *Davis' Joyful Meeting in Glory* (1919). Albert E. Brumley's arrangement is © 1937 (*Brumley's Radio Favorites*). The best-known recording is by Jim Reeves (1962).
- URL: https://www.hymnary.org/hymn/PHSS2012/715
- Confidence: VERIFIED. Correction: it is often credited to Brumley as author, but he was only the arranger.

## 4. Survey data on prosperity and healing

4.1 **Pew Forum, "Spirit and Power: A 10-Country Survey of Pentecostals" (released 5 Oct 2006, Templeton-funded).**
- Countries: US, Brazil, Chile, Guatemala, Kenya, Nigeria, South Africa, India, Philippines and South Korea.
- Executive summary, verbatim:
  - "Majorities of pentecostals in all 10 countries surveyed agree that God will grant good health and relief from sickness to believers who have enough faith, and in nine of the countries most pentecostals say that God will grant material prosperity to all believers who have enough faith."
  - "In all 10 countries surveyed, large majorities of pentecostals (ranging from 56% in South Korea to 87% in Kenya) say that they have personally experienced or witnessed the divine healing of an illness or injury."
  - Renewalists (Pentecostals plus charismatics) make up 5% of the population in the surveyed areas of India and 60% in Guatemala.
- URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2006/10/05/spirit-and-power/ ; topline: https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/2006/10/pentecostals-topline-06.pdf
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the quotes above.

4.2 **Pew 2006, prosperity belief by country (as reported by Christianity Today).**
- Figure: Pentecostals agreeing God grants material prosperity to all believers with enough faith: Nigeria 95%, South Africa 90%, Kenya 85%.
- Other secondary sources give different numbers (Nigeria 96, Kenya 83, SA 80), so use the topline for exact figures.
- The one country without a majority is not named in the summary; it is likely South Korea or the US. NOT VERIFIED.
- URL: https://www.christianitytoday.com/2007/07/gospel-riches/
- Confidence: PARTIAL.

4.3 **Pew, "Religion in Latin America" (Nov 2014; about 30,000 interviews, Oct 2013–Feb 2014).**
- Figure: majorities of Protestants in every country agree that "God will grant wealth and good health to believers who have enough faith", ranging from 56% (Brazil) to 91% (Venezuela).
- Catholics are also high: Guatemala 89%, Panama 86%, El Salvador 84%, Honduras 84%, Nicaragua 78%.
- URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2014/11/13/chapter-4-pentecostalism/
- Confidence: VERIFIED (via search extract of the Pew page).
- Caution: these numbers are often wrongly cited as "Pew 2006".

4.4 **US data.**
- TIME poll (Sept 2006, "Does God Want You to Be Rich?", by Van Biema and Chu):
  - 17% of Christians identify with the prosperity movement.
  - 61% believe God wants people to be prosperous.
  - 31% say giving money to God brings more money back.
  - 21% say material wealth is a sign of God's blessing.
  - URL: https://albertmohler.com/?p=772 ; https://christianpost.com/news/most-christians-believe-god-wants-people-to-be-rich.html
  - Confidence: VERIFIED (secondary reports of the TIME poll).
- Lifeway Research (2017 fieldwork, published July 2018; 1,010 Protestant churchgoers):
  - 69% agree "God wants me to prosper financially".
  - 38% say "my church teaches that if I give more money... God will bless me in return".
  - URL: https://news.lifeway.com/2018/07/31/most-churchgoers-say-god-wants-them-to-prosper-financially/
  - Confidence: VERIFIED.
- Lifeway 2022 churchgoer survey: 76% and 52% on the same two items. Lifeway Sept 2024 pastors' survey: 37% of Protestant pastors say God wants people to prosper financially.
  - URL: https://research.lifeway.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Lifeway-Pastors-Sept-2024-Prosperity-Gospel-Report.pdf
  - Confidence: VERIFIED (secondary).
- I found no dedicated Pew 2008 or 2014 US prosperity item. A secondary claim that 46% of religious Americans agree with the prosperity statement (and 56% with the health statement) probably comes from the Pew 2006 US general-population sample. NOT VERIFIED.

## 5. Word of Faith history

5.1 **E.W. Kenyon (1867–1948).**
- Figure: Kenyon attended Emerson College of Oratory in Boston (c. 1892), a hub of New Thought. His "positive confession" and "revelation knowledge" ideas were later taken up by Hagin.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._W._Kenyon
- Confidence: PARTIAL. The dates are standard; the Emerson detail is reported by McConnell and Wikipedia.

5.2 **D.R. McConnell, *A Different Gospel* (Hendrickson, 1988; rev. 1995).**
- Thesis: Kenneth Hagin plagiarised Kenyon extensively, with side-by-side passages. Kenyon in turn absorbed New Thought, Christian Science and Unity metaphysics. So Word of Faith is "metaphysical" in origin, not Pentecostal.
- Counterpoint: Dale Simmons (*E.W. Kenyon and the Postbellum Pursuit of Peace, Power, and Plenty*, 1997) and others argue that Kenyon's main roots were the Higher Life and faith-cure movements (A.B. Simpson, A.J. Gordon), with only partial New Thought influence. The plagiarism finding stands; the New Thought genealogy is DISPUTED in its strong form.
- URL: https://www.equip.org/articles/a-different-gospel ; https://bookreviews.icsahome.com/book-reviews/book-review-a-different-gospel-csj-6-1
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the thesis.

5.3 **Kenneth E. Hagin (1917–2003).**
- Figure: founded Rhema Bible Training Center (now College) in 1974 in Tulsa, which later moved to Broken Arrow, Oklahoma.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_E._Hagin
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

5.4 **Kenneth Copeland.**
- Figure: Kenneth and Gloria Copeland founded Kenneth Copeland Ministries in 1967 in Fort Worth, after Hagin seminars and a period as Oral Roberts's pilot and chauffeur at ORU.
- Confidence: PARTIAL (1967 founding seen in a search extract).

5.5 **Kate Bowler, *Blessed: A History of the American Prosperity Gospel* (Oxford UP, 2013).**
- Figure: Bowler organises the movement around four themes: faith (as an activating power), wealth, health and victory.
- She distinguishes "hard prosperity" (explicit financial and health guarantees, tithing as a formula; Copeland, Hinn) from "soft prosperity" (therapeutic, self-esteem, "best life"; Osteen, Joyce Meyer, T.D. Jakes). Soft prosperity became dominant from the 1990s.
- Roots: Pentecostal healing revivals plus New Thought plus American pragmatism.
- Her own count, from memory: 115 US prosperity megachurches (2,000+ attenders).
- URL: https://www.9marks.org/review/blessed-history-american-prosperity-gospel/ ; https://www.new-directions.sps.ed.ac.uk/blessed-a-history-of-the-american-prosperity-gospel-book-review/
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the categories. The 115-megachurch count is NOT VERIFIED (from memory).

## 6. Lausanne statement on the prosperity gospel

6.1 **Lausanne Theology Working Group, Africa chapter.**
- Dates: consultations in Akropong, Ghana, 8–9 Oct 2008 and 1–4 Sept 2009. First draft by Chris Wright, edited with John Azumah and Kwabena Asamoah-Gyadu. Reprinted in Christianity Today (Dec 2009) and *Evangelical Review of Theology* 34:2 (Apr 2010), pp. 99–102.
- Definition: the prosperity gospel is "the teaching that believers have a right to the blessings of health and wealth and that they can obtain these blessings through positive confessions of faith and the 'sowing of seeds' through the faithful payments of tithes and offerings".
- Affirmations:
  - God's miraculous power.
  - The growth of churches.
  - Biblical teaching on material welfare.
  - Rejection of a sacred/secular dualism.
- Rejections:
  - The "automatic" or manipulative use of faith and words.
  - Prosperity as a measure of God's favour.
  - Poverty or sickness as a sign of curse or lack of faith.
  - Leaders' unaccountable lifestyles.
- Conclusion: the "excessive and unbalanced teachings of many prosperity teachers" are "false teaching" and in its extreme forms "another gospel".
- URL: https://lausanne.org/akropong ; https://theology.worldea.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/ERT-34-2.pdf ; https://acfar.org/resources/articles/a-statement-on-prosperity-teaching
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the summary. The exact quoted phrases are PARTIAL (lausanne.org could not be fetched); check them against the ERT PDF.

## 7. Latter Rain, Manifest Sons, dominion theology

7.1 **Latter Rain revival.**
- Figure: began 11–14 Feb 1948 at Sharon Orphanage and Schools, North Battleford, Saskatchewan. Leaders were George and Ern Hawtin and Percy Hunt, inspired by William Branham's Vancouver meetings in late 1947.
- Features: impartation of gifts by laying on of hands, personal prophecy, restoration of apostles and prophets.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latter_Rain_(post%E2%80%93World_War_II_movement) ; https://revival-library.org/histories/1947-1958-latter-rain-revival-intro/
- Confidence: VERIFIED. The venue is disputed between Sharon and the local Foursquare church; Sharon is standard.

7.2 **George Warnock, *The Feast of Tabernacles* (1951).**
- Figure: reads Israel's three feasts as stages of church history. The last stage (Tabernacles) brings a matured company of "overcomers" or "manifested sons of God" (from Rom 8:19) who attain spiritual and even bodily perfection before Christ's return.
- Related ideas (Branham, the Hawtins) predate the book, so Warnock systematised the doctrine rather than inventing it.
- URL: https://william-branham.org/research/george_warnock
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

7.3 **Assemblies of God, 23rd General Council, Seattle, Sept 1949.**
- Figure: Resolution 7, "The New Order of the Latter Rain", disapproves of teachings including:
  - "excessive importance" on imparting gifts by laying on of hands and on prophecy;
  - the church being built on present-day apostles and prophets;
  - confession of sin to man and deliverance as practised;
  - impartation of languages for missionary service;
  - "such other wrestings and distortions of Scripture".
- "New Order" was an opponents' label.
- URL: https://news.ag.org/en/articles/news/2025/10/this-week-in-ag-history-oct-15-1949
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

7.4 **Seven Mountain Mandate.**
- Origin: Loren Cunningham (YWAM) and Bill Bright (Campus Crusade) say they each received a list of seven "spheres" in 1975 (family, religion, education, government, media, arts and entertainment, business). Francis Schaeffer is said to have taught similarly, but his presence at the meeting is DISPUTED.
- The "mountains" language and the dominionist turn came from Lance Wallnau (about 2000 onward) and C. Peter Wagner (NAR, from about the late 1990s), and was popularised in *Invading Babylon: The 7 Mountain Mandate* (Wallnau and Bill Johnson, 2013).
- Original intent was evangelistic influence; the NAR version is "taking dominion".
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Mountain_Mandate ; https://ebsco.com/research-starters/religion-and-philosophy/seven-mountain-mandate
- Confidence: VERIFIED. The 1975 vision rests on the participants' own testimony.

7.5 **Abraham Kuyper, "Souvereiniteit in eigen kring" (Sphere Sovereignty).**
- Delivered 20 Oct 1880 in the Nieuwe Kerk, Amsterdam, at the opening of the Free University.
- Quote: "there is not a square inch in the whole domain of our human existence over which Christ, who is Sovereign over all, does not cry: 'Mine!'" (tr. Bratt, *Abraham Kuyper: A Centennial Reader*, 1998, p. 488).
- Note: Kuyper's sphere sovereignty limits the church and the state to their own spheres. It is the opposite of a theocratic takeover, so its use by 7M advocates is contested.
- URL: https://bredenhof.ca/2018/03/20/quotable-church-history-not-a-square-inch/ ; https://www.thebanner.org/columns/2017/09/church-doctrine
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

## 8. Cessationism and continuationism

8.1 **B.B. Warfield, *Counterfeit Miracles* (Scribner's, 1918).**
- Figure: the Thomas Smyth Lectures at Columbia Theological Seminary, SC, delivered Oct 1917. Chapter 1 is "The Cessation of the Charismata".
- Thesis: the miraculous gifts were "the credentials of the Apostles", passed on only through apostolic hands, and so ceased with the apostolic generation. Later chapters critique patristic, medieval and Roman Catholic miracles, Irvingism, faith-healing and mind-cure.
- URL: https://archive.org/download/counterfeitmir00warf/counterfeitmir00warf_bw.pdf
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

8.2 **Jack Deere, *Surprised by the Power of the Spirit* (Zondervan, 1993; 256 pp.).**
- Figure: Deere is a former associate professor of OT at Dallas Theological Seminary who became continuationist through John Wimber and the Vineyard. He argues that cessationism rests on experience (the absence of miracles), not on Scripture.
- Rebuttal: Richard Mayhue, *Master's Seminary Journal* 5:2 (1994).
- URL: https://www.galaxie.com/article/bsac151-602-08 ; https://www.galaxie.com/article/tmsj05-2-02
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

8.3 **Craig S. Keener, *Miracles: The Credibility of the New Testament Accounts* (Baker Academic, 2011).**
- Size: 2 volumes, xxxviii + 1,172 pp.
- Thesis, in Keener's words: "eyewitnesses do offer miracle claims". A secondary thesis is that supernatural explanations should be "welcome on the scholarly table". The book rebuts Hume's appeal to "uniform experience" with hundreds of modern cases from around the world. It won a CT Book Award (2013).
- URL: https://denverjournal.denverseminary.edu/?p=10646 ; https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/themelios/review/miracles-the-credibility-of-the-new-testament-accounts/
- Confidence: VERIFIED. Page counts vary by listing (1,248 counts the front matter).

## 9. Prayer and healing studies

9.1 **Brown, Mory, Williams and McClymond, "Study of the Therapeutic Effects of Proximal Intercessory Prayer (STEPP) on Auditory and Visual Impairments in Rural Mozambique."**
- Publication: *Southern Medical Journal* 103(9), Sept 2010, pp. 864–869.
- Design: 24 subjects (14 hearing-impaired, 11 vision-impaired), tested with an audiometer and vision charts before and after Pentecostal (Iris Ministries) hands-on prayer.
- Results: significant improvement in hearing (p < 0.003) and vision (p < 0.02). Three subjects went from 20/400 or worse to 20/80 or better.
- Limits: no control group, no blinding, small sample. The authors themselves allowed that suggestion or placebo may play a part. Critics (e.g. Steven Novella) call it a pilot at best.
- URL: https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/items/ad8f6faa-1a4f-4a8a-9638-2578c33c1f1e ; https://theness.com/neurologicablog/proximal-intercessory-prayer/
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

9.2 **STEP: Benson et al., *American Heart Journal* 151(4):934–942, April 2006.**
- Design: 1,802 CABG patients at 6 US hospitals, prayed for at a distance for 14 days by three Christian groups.
- Results (complications within 30 days):
  - Prayed for, told uncertain: 52% (315/604).
  - Not prayed for, told uncertain: 51% (304/597). RR 1.02.
  - Prayed for, told certain: 59% (352/601). RR 1.14 versus uncertain.
- So distant intercessory prayer showed no benefit, and knowing one was prayed for was associated with more complications.
- Note: this tests distant, anonymous prayer by strangers, not proximal prayer.
- URL: https://www.aafp.org/afp/2006/0901/p824.html ; https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2006/04/prayers-dont-help-heart-surgery-patients/
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

9.3 **Lourdes recognised miracles.**
- 70th: Sr Bernadette Moriau, declared 11 Feb 2018.

## 10. Christianity, healthcare, poverty

10.1 **Basil of Caesarea's Basileias ("Basiliad").**
- Figure: built just outside Caesarea in Cappadocia from about 369–372. Basil was made bishop in 370, but the project began during the 368–369 famine while he was still a presbyter. Letter 94 (372/3) defends it.
- It housed a hospital with physicians, a hospice for lepers, a poorhouse, an orphanage and a hostel. Gregory of Nazianzus (Oration 43, c. 381) compared it to the Seven Wonders.
- URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basileias ; https://christianhistoryinstitute.org/magazine/article/basils-house-of-healing ; https://hekint.org/2017/02/24/reconstructing-the-worlds-first-hospital-the-basiliad/
- Confidence: VERIFIED. The "first hospital" label is qualified: it is the first well-documented public hospital for the sick poor, and Roman valetudinaria (military or slave infirmaries) existed earlier.

10.2 **Rodney Stark, *The Rise of Christianity* (Princeton UP, 1996), ch. 4.**
- Epidemics: the Antonine plague (165–180) and the plague of Cyprian (c. 249–262).
- Evidence: Dionysius of Alexandria's Easter letter (c. 260) praises Christians who nursed the sick at the cost of their own lives, while pagans "fled from their dearest".
- Stark's estimate: elementary nursing "would have cut mortality by two-thirds or even more" (citing W.H. McNeill).
- Critique: Bart Ehrman calls the thesis "almost certainly wrong" in its demographic form.
- URL: https://www.biola.edu/blogs/good-book-blog/2020/how-did-early-christians-respond-to-plagues ; https://ehrmanblog.org/superior-health-care-as-an-explanation-for-the-spread-of-christianity/
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the Dionysius text. The two-thirds figure is Stark's modelled estimate, not data; treat as DISPUTED.

10.3 **Robert D. Woodberry, "The Missionary Roots of Liberal Democracy", *APSR* 106(2), May 2012, pp. 244–274.**
- Finding: "conversionary Protestants" (missionaries independent of state churches) catalysed mass education, printing, newspapers, voluntary associations, religious liberty and colonial reforms. The historic prevalence of Protestant missions explains about half the variation in democracy across Africa, Asia, Latin America and Oceania, and survives 50+ controls and instrumental-variable tests. The article won APSA awards.
- Challenge: Nikolova and Polansky, *BJPolS* (2020/21), "Conversionary Protestants Do Not Cause Democracy". With 26 alternative democracy measures the effect is not robust.
- URL: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/missionary-roots-of-liberal-democracy/3D96CF5CB2F7FEB19B1835393D084B9A
- Confidence: VERIFIED. The finding itself is DISPUTED.

10.4 **Max Weber, *Die protestantische Ethik und der Geist des Kapitalismus*.**
- Published in two parts in *Archiv für Sozialwissenschaft* (1904–05); revised 1920; English translation by Parsons (1930).
- Critiques: Tawney (1926); historians noting capitalism in Catholic Italy before the Reformation.
- Becker and Woessmann, "Was Weber Wrong? A Human Capital Theory of Protestant Economic History", *QJE* 124(2), 2009. Using data from 19th-century Prussian counties, they find Protestants' higher literacy (Bible reading) accounts for most of the Protestant economic advantage. The literacy mechanism replaces the "work ethic".
- URL: https://www.diw.de/de/diw_01.c.763684.de/s_5368.html ; https://cesifo.org/DocDL/cesifo1_wp1987.pdf
- Confidence: VERIFIED. Correction: the "1905" date is fine for part 2, but the essay is properly dated 1904–05.

10.5 **World Bank extreme poverty.**
- Old $2.15 line (2017 PPP): about 38% (1990) to about 8.4–8.5% (2019). The 2019 figure was revised from 8.7% to 8.4%. The ~38% and 8.5% figures come from the World Bank's *Poverty and Shared Prosperity 2022* report.
- New line from June 2025: $3.00/day (2021 PPP). This raises every year's level (2022 adds about 125m people), but the downward trend stays.
- URL: https://datahelpdesk.worldbank.org/knowledgebase/articles/746163-w ; https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/factsheet/2025/06/05/june-2025-update-to-global-poverty-lines ; https://pip.worldbank.org
- Confidence: PARTIAL.
- Correction: "~9% in 2019" is slightly high under the $2.15 line (8.4–8.5%). Under the new $3.00 line, the 1990 and 2019 numbers differ again; pull them from PIP before publishing.

## 11. Healing accounts in the Gospels

11.1 **Count of Jesus' healings.**
- No authoritative scholarly count exists; it depends on whether parallels, exorcisms and resuscitations are counted.
- Common figures: about 37 total miracles in the four Gospels, healings plus nature miracles (Wartburg Project chart). About 40 descriptions of healings counting parallels. About 20–26 distinct individual or group healing events, about 17–20 excluding exorcisms.
- The "~41" figure appears in some popular lists that count parallels separately.
- URL: https://wartburgproject.org/documents/miracles-chart.pdf ; https://www.jesusfilm.org/blog/all-the-healings/ ; https://www.soulshepherding.org/article/jesus-healings-and-miracles/
- Confidence: DISPUTED (method-dependent). Recommend saying "roughly two dozen distinct healing accounts, plus many summary statements".

11.2 **Summary statements that Jesus healed "all".**
- Matt 4:23–24 ("healing every disease and every affliction... and he healed them").
- Matt 8:16 ("healed all who were sick").
- Matt 12:15 ("he healed them all").
- Luke 6:19 ("power came out from him and healed them all").
- Acts 10:38 ("healing all who were oppressed by the devil").
- Also Matt 14:36 and Luke 4:40.
- Counterweight: Mark 6:5 (he could do no mighty work at Nazareth "except that he laid his hands on a few sick people") and John 5:3–9 (one man healed among a "multitude").
- Confidence: VERIFIED (text, ESV).

## 12. Prosperity teaching: harms and benefits

12.1 **Paul Gifford, *Ghana's New Christianity: Pentecostalism in a Globalizing African Economy* (Indiana UP, 2004).**
- Thesis: Accra's charismatic megachurches are dominated by the faith/prosperity gospel and "prophetic" deliverance. Gifford argues that this enchanted, success-centred worldview does not promote the work ethic or the structural and political change that development needs. It may divert attention from accountability and productive investment (contrast with Weber).
- Note: Gifford is sympathetic but critical.
- URL: https://www.new-directions.sps.ed.ac.uk/ghanas-new-christianity-book-review/ ; https://muse.jhu.edu/article/203481/pdf
- Confidence: VERIFIED in general terms. Exact wording is PARTIAL.

12.2 **Donald E. Miller and Tetsunao Yamamori, *Global Pentecostalism: The New Face of Christian Social Engagement* (Univ. of California Press, 2007).**
- Design: 4 years of fieldwork in 20 developing countries. Of more than 400 nominations of innovative, socially active fast-growing churches, about 85% were Pentecostal.
- Concept: "Progressive Pentecostalism", which meets "the spiritual, physical, and social needs" of communities (the "integral gospel"). The authors distinguish it from prosperity teaching.
- URL: https://catholicbooksreview.org/2008/miller.htm ; https://www.ucpress.edu/books/global-pentecostalism/pdf
- Confidence: VERIFIED.

12.3 **Bryan, Choi and Karlan, "Randomizing Religion: The Impact of Protestant Evangelism on Economic Outcomes", *QJE* 136(1), 2021 (NBER WP 24278, 2018).**
- Design: 6,276 ultra-poor Filipino households; randomised invitations to International Care Ministries' evangelical values and theology course.
- Results: at 6 months, higher religiosity and higher income (about 9%), possibly via "grit". No change in consumption or food security, and lower perceived relative status. Most effects faded by 30 months.
- Note: this course was not prosperity teaching.
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w24278.pdf ; https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/can-religious-teachings-help-lift-people-out-of-poverty
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the design and direction. The about-9% figure is PARTIAL.

12.4 **Other prosperity-specific evidence.**
- A US lab study found that prosperity messages raised optimistic bias and financial risk-taking.
- A Kenyan descriptive survey (2024) reported self-perceived entrepreneurial gains.
- A randomised trial of prosperity-gospel workshops in Africa is registered (AEA RCT Registry #13373, n ≈ 1,400), but I found no results.
- Pew 2006: in most countries, Pentecostals are "somewhat more hopeful" about their financial future.
- URL: https://www.socialscienceregistry.org/trials/13373
- Confidence: PARTIAL. There is no robust causal evidence either way on prosperity teaching itself.

12.5 **Peter Berger and the "Pentecostal ethic".**
- Berger argued that Pentecostalism functions as a functional equivalent of Weber's Protestant ethic in the Global South (e.g. "Max Weber is alive and well, and living in Guatemala", 2010). Related: David Martin, *Tongues of Fire* (1990), and Bernice Martin.
- I did not verify a specific text or URL. Confidence: NOT VERIFIED.
- Robert Barro (with McCleary) studied religion and growth (e.g. "Religion and Economic Growth", *ASR* 2003: belief in heaven or hell correlates with growth, church attendance correlates negatively). This is not a Pentecostal study, so don't cite it as one. Confidence: PARTIAL (from memory).
