# Research notes: three topics (compiled 2026-10-24)
Key: VERIFIED = confirmed this session by search or a primary source. PARTIAL = right in substance, but some detail is off, needs a caveat or wasn't checked. DISPUTED = scholars disagree. NOT VERIFIED = not found.
Note: Entries marked "(recall)" come from standard reference knowledge and were not re-searched this session. Check the URL before you publish.

## A. Bible used to defend atrocities

A1. SBC founded over slavery; 1995 apology.
- The SBC was founded in Augusta, GA, in May 1845. It split from northern Baptists after the Home Mission Society refused to appoint slaveholders as missionaries.
- 1995 Atlanta convention (150th anniversary), "Resolution on Racial Reconciliation": "we apologize to all African-Americans for condoning and/or perpetuating individual and systemic racism in our lifetime."
- Source: sbc.net resolutions, 1995. https://www.sbc.net/resource-library/resolutions/resolution-on-racial-reconciliation-on-the-150th-anniversary-of-the-southern-baptist-convention/ . VERIFIED (recall)

A2. The "Slave Bible" (1807).
- Full title: "Parts of the Holy Bible, selected for the use of the Negro Slaves, in the British West-India Islands." Printed in London in 1807 for the Society for the Conversion of Negro Slaves.
- Museum of the Bible curator Anthony Schmidt: "About 90 percent of the Old Testament is missing [and] 50 percent of the New Testament is missing."
- It kept 232 of 1,189 chapters (NPR). Christian Post reported 282, probably a misprint.
- The Exodus liberation narrative is omitted. Eph 6:5 ("Servants, be obedient...") is retained.
- Only 3 copies are known (2 in the UK, 1 at the Museum of the Bible). Exhibit date: NPR reported it in Dec 2018; the exhibit opened in late Nov 2018.
- Sources: NPR, 2018. https://www.npr.org/2018/12/09/674995075/slave-bible-from-the-1800s-omitted-key-passages-that-could-incite-rebellion . Smithsonian, 2018. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/heavily-abridged-slave-bible-removed-passages-might-encourage-uprisings-180970989/ . VERIFIED (use 232, not 282)

A3. "Curse of Ham" (Gen 9:20-27).
- Correction: the text curses CANAAN, not Ham ("Cursed be Canaan; a servant of servants shall he be", Gen 9:25).
- Links between the curse and dark skin appear in late-antique and medieval Jewish, Christian and Islamic writings (e.g. Babylonian Talmud Sanhedrin 108b; medieval Arabic sources). Goldenberg argues the rabbinic texts were later misread as racial.
- It was used to justify the Atlantic slave trade and US slavery (17th-19th c.), and later segregation.
- Decline: it lost mainstream respectability after the Civil Rights era (1950s-60s). Related reversals: the LDS priesthood ban, partly linked to "Cain/Ham" ideas, ended in 1978. The SBC 1995 resolution also rejected such readings.
- Source: David M. Goldenberg, "The Curse of Ham: Race and Slavery in Early Judaism, Christianity, and Islam" (Princeton UP, 2003). Stephen Haynes, "Noah's Curse" (OUP, 2002). PARTIAL (no single decline date; "mid-20th c." is safe)

A4. Proof texts and Douglass.
- Pro-slavery texts: Eph 6:5, Col 3:22, 1 Pet 2:18, 1 Tim 6:1-2, Lev 25:44-46, Philemon (Onesimus sent back), Gen 9:25. All are standard in 1800s US sermons (e.g. Thornton Stringfellow, "A Brief Examination of Scripture Testimony on the Institution of Slavery", 1841/1850).
- Abolitionist counter-texts: Ex 21:16 ("he that stealeth a man... shall surely be put to death"), Deut 23:15-16 (do not return an escaped slave), Gal 3:28, Acts 17:26, Matt 7:12 (Golden Rule).
- Douglass, Narrative (1845), Appendix: "between the Christianity of this land, and the Christianity of Christ, I recognize the widest possible difference... I love the pure, peaceable, and impartial Christianity of Christ: I therefore hate the corrupt, slaveholding, women-whipping, cradle-plundering, partial and hypocritical Christianity of this land."
- Source: Douglass 1845. https://docsouth.unc.edu/neh/douglass/douglass.html . Mark Noll, "The Civil War as a Theological Crisis" (2006). VERIFIED (recall; quote is the well-known text)

A5. Dutch Reformed Church (NGK) and apartheid.
- 1974 report "Ras, Volk en Nasie" gave apartheid a biblical defence.
- 1982, Ottawa: the World Alliance of Reformed Churches declared apartheid a sin and its theological justification a heresy, and suspended the NGK.
- 1982: the Belhar Confession was drafted by the (Coloured) Dutch Reformed Mission Church. It was formally adopted in 1986.
- 1986 "Kerk en Samelewing" (Church and Society): this was a partial retreat. It said apartheid could not be justified from Scripture, opened membership to all races and dropped the biblical ban on mixed marriages. Critics (e.g. Sojourners) saw it as still giving qualified support. A conservative split followed: the Afrikaanse Protestantse Kerk was formed in 1987.
- 1990: the General Synod called any biblical defence a "serious errancy" and declared apartheid a sin. At Rustenburg (Nov 1990), Prof. Willie Jonker publicly confessed on the church's behalf.
- Correction: treat 1986 as the first step and 1990 as the full renunciation.
- Sources: Sojourners, 1991. https://www.sojo.net/magazine/february-march-1991/apartheid-sin . SciELO SHE vol 39. https://www.scielo.org.za/pdf/she/v39n1/10.pdf . VERIFIED

A6. Crusades.
- Urban II preached at the Council of Clermont on 27 Nov 1095; the crowd cried "Deus vult" (Robert the Monk's account).
- Jerusalem fell on 15 July 1099 and a massacre of Muslims and Jews followed.
- Death toll: chroniclers' figures (e.g. Ibn al-Athir's 70,000) are seen as inflated. Kedar (2004) suggests a few thousand to ~10,000+.
- Source: Thomas Asbridge, "The First Crusade" (2004). B.Z. Kedar, "The Jerusalem Massacre of July 1099", Crusades 3 (2004). PARTIAL (dates verified; casualty figures disputed)

A7. Doctrine of Discovery.
- Dum Diversas (Nicholas V, 1452) let Portugal "reduce to perpetual slavery" Saracens and pagans.
- Romanus Pontifex (1455) extended it.
- Inter caetera (Alexander VI, 1493) divided New World lands for Spain.
- 30 March 2023: the Dicasteries for Culture & Education and for Promoting Integral Human Development issued a "Joint Note on the Doctrine of Discovery". It said the bulls "did not adequately reflect the equal dignity and rights of Indigenous peoples" and "have never been considered expressions of the Catholic faith." Correction: it repudiates the "Doctrine of Discovery" concept but did NOT formally rescind the bulls.
- Source: Vatican Press Office, 2023. https://press.vatican.va/content/salastampa/en/bollettino/pubblico/2023/03/30/230330b.html . VERIFIED (recall)

A8. European witch trials.
- Modern consensus: ~40,000-60,000 executions, c.1450-1750. Levack estimates ~45,000 from ~90,000 trials; Briggs gives 40-50k. The "9 million" figure is a myth. About 75-80% of victims were women. Half or more were in the Holy Roman Empire.
- Ex 22:18 (KJV): "Thou shalt not suffer a witch to live."
- Malleus Maleficarum: Heinrich Kramer (Institoris), 1486, first printed 1486/87 (Speyer). It followed Innocent VIII's bull Summis desiderantes (1484). Jakob Sprenger's co-authorship is disputed.
- Source: Brian Levack, "The Witch-Hunt in Early Modern Europe" (3rd ed. 2006). VERIFIED (recall)

A9. Spanish Inquisition (1478-1834).
- Henningsen & Contreras studied 44,674 trials (1540-1700). They found 1,604 death sentences: 826 executed in person and 778 in effigy (straw figures, not real deaths), i.e. under 2% of trials.
- Kamen estimates ~2,000 executions before 1530. Monter estimates ~1,000 for 1530-1630 and ~250 for 1630-1730.
- Total: ~3,000-5,000 executions. Llorente's 31,912 is regarded as exaggerated.
- Source: Henningsen & Contreras 1986. Henry Kamen, "The Spanish Inquisition" (4th ed. 2014). https://www.ereticopedia.org/number-of-victims-spanish-inquisition . VERIFIED

A10. Luther and the Jews.
- "Von den Juden und ihren Lügen" (On the Jews and Their Lies), 1543. It urged burning synagogues and schools and destroying homes.
- The Nazis used it: reprinted; Streicher cited Luther at Nuremberg; Bishop Martin Sasse published excerpts after Kristallnacht (1938).
- ELCA "Declaration to the Jewish Community", 18 April 1994: "we reject this violent invective... [and] express our deep and abiding sorrow over its tragic effects."
- Nostra Aetate (Vatican II), 28 Oct 1965, §4: what happened in Christ's passion "cannot be charged against all the Jews, without distinction, then alive, nor against the Jews of today."
- Sources: https://www.elca.org/faith/ecumenical-and-inter-religious-relations/inter-religious-relations/jewish-relations . https://www.vatican.va/archive/hist_councils/ii_vatican_council/documents/vat-ii_decl_19651028_nostra-aetate_en.html . VERIFIED (recall)

A11. Thirty Years' War (1618-48).
- The range of 4.5-8 million deaths is widely cited. Peter H. Wilson estimates ~5 million+ in the Empire (≈20% of its population): "1.8 million soldiers, at least 3.2 million civilians." Most deaths came from disease and famine.
- It was a mixed religious and political war (Catholic France allied with Protestant Sweden).
- Sources: Wilson, "Europe's Tragedy" (2009). Gresham lecture, 2018. https://www.gresham.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2018-05-23_PeterWilson_ThirtyYearsWar-T.pdf . VERIFIED (range is consensus, not Wilson's own)

A12. Galileo.
- The Holy Office condemned him on 22 June 1633 ("vehemently suspect of heresy"); he was sentenced to house arrest.
- 31 Oct 1992: John Paul II addressed the Pontifical Academy of Sciences, accepting the commission's (1981-92) findings that Galileo's judges had erred.
- Correction: JPII acknowledged an "error"/"tragic mutual incomprehension". It is commonly called a "rehabilitation" but was not a formal apology.
- Source: L'Osservatore Romano, 4 Nov 1992. NYT, 31 Oct 1992. https://www.nytimes.com/1992/10/31/world/after-350-years-vatican-says-galileo-was-right-it-moves.html . VERIFIED (recall)

A13. Rwanda 1994.
- Death toll: ~500,000-800,000 Tutsi and moderate Hutu killed in ~100 days (Rwandan government census: 1,074,017).
- Churches were major massacre sites: Ntarama (~5,000), Nyamata (~10,000), Nyange.
- Fr. Athanase Seromba ordered the bulldozing of Nyange church on ~2,000 sheltering Tutsi. The ICTR convicted him and raised his sentence to life on appeal (2008).
- Two Benedictine nuns (Sisters Gertrude and Kizito) were convicted in Belgium in 2001.
- 20 March 2017: Pope Francis met Kagame and "implored anew God's forgiveness for the sins and failings of the Church and its members... [who] succumbed to hatred and violence."
- Source: Vatican Press Office, 2017. https://www.bbc.com/news/world-africa-39331159 . VERIFIED (recall)

A14. Bob Jones University.
- Bob Jones Univ. v. United States, 461 U.S. 574, was decided 24 May 1983 (8-1). The IRS could revoke tax exemption over racially discriminatory policies; BJU had argued the Bible forbids interracial dating.
- The ban was dropped in March 2000. Bob Jones III announced it on Larry King Live after the George W. Bush campaign visit controversy.
- The university apologized in Nov 2008.
- Source: https://www.oyez.org/cases/1982/81-3 . NYT, 4 Mar 2000. VERIFIED (recall)

A15. Other things defended by Scripture.
- a) Marital rape. The common-law "marital exemption" comes from Sir Matthew Hale (1736): a wife "hath given up herself in this kind unto her husband." Nebraska first removed it in 1976. By 5 July 1993 (North Carolina) it was a crime in all 50 states, though many states kept lesser penalties or exceptions afterwards. Biblical "submission" (Eph 5:22) and 1 Cor 7:4-5 have been cited to discourage refusal; this is a documented pastoral pattern, not law. Source: Bergen, "Wife Rape" (1996). Finkelhor & Yllö (1985). VERIFIED (recall) for the 1993 date. PARTIAL for the scriptural framing.
- b) Anesthesia in childbirth: largely a myth. James Young Simpson used chloroform from Nov 1847 and wrote "Answer to the Religious Objections Advanced Against the Employment of Anaesthetic Agents in Midwifery" (Dec 1847), reinterpreting Gen 3:16. A.D. Farr, "Early opposition to obstetric anaesthesia", Anaesthesia 35(9), 1980 (not 1983), called religious opposition "a myth of historiography": objections were mostly medical. A later Edinburgh case-book study suggests some resistance existed. Queen Victoria's use of chloroform (1853) ended debate. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7006372/ (Farr 1980). DISPUTED (say "some clergy objected; how widespread is disputed")
- c) Lightning rods. Rev. Thomas Prince (Boston) suggested Franklin's rods caused the 1755 Cape Ann earthquake by driving "electrical substance" into the earth. Some in Europe called rods impious. The popular framing comes from A.D. White's "Warfare of Science with Theology" (1896), whose conflict thesis historians now criticise. Source: I. Bernard Cohen, "Benjamin Franklin's Science" (1990). PARTIAL
- d) Segregation sermons. Examples: W.A. Criswell's 1956 South Carolina speech (he recanted in the late 1960s); Bob Jones Sr., "Is Segregation Scriptural?" (Easter 1960 radio sermon). Acts 17:26 ("bounds of their habitation") and Gen 11 (Babel) were common proof texts. Source: Jane Dailey, "Sex, Segregation, and the Sacred after Brown", J. Am. Hist. 91 (2004). VERIFIED (recall)
- e) "Spare the rod" (Prov 13:24, 23:13-14). The phrase "spare the rod and spoil the child" itself is from Samuel Butler's Hudibras (1663/64), not the Bible. Gershoff & Grogan-Kaylor's 2016 meta-analysis (J. Family Psychology; 75 studies, ~160,000 children) linked spanking to more aggression and mental-health problems, with no benefit. VERIFIED (recall)

A16. Abuse cover-ups.
- Guidepost Solutions report on the SBC Executive Committee, released 22 May 2022 (covers 2000-2021).
- Ex-general counsel D. August Boto and spokesman Roger Oldham kept a private list of accused ministers. On release (26-27 May 2022) it was a 205-page list with "more than 700 entries" (AP; ~703 is the commonly quoted count), mostly cases from 2000-2019.
- Guidepost found no indication anyone acted "to ensure that the accused ministers were no longer in positions of power."
- Boto's 2019 email called survivor advocacy "a satanic scheme to completely distract us from evangelism."
- Houston Chronicle/SA Express-News "Abuse of Faith" (Feb 2019): ~380 SBC leaders/volunteers accused since 1998, ~700+ victims.
- Matthew 18 / "touch not mine anointed" (Ps 105:15): these are documented as silencing tools in survivor and GRACE (Boz Tchividjian) reporting. No quantitative data were found.
- Sources: AP/RNS, 27 May 2022. https://religionnews.com/2022/05/27/southern-baptist-leaders-release-secret-accused-abuser-list/ . https://www.sbc.net/?p=39722 . VERIFIED for the >700 figure. NOT VERIFIED for any statistic on Matt 18 misuse.

## B. Apologizing when not at fault

B1. Schumann & Ross (2010).
- Psychological Science 21(11):1649-55, "Why women apologize more than men: Gender differences in thresholds for perceiving offensive behavior."
- In a diary study, women apologized more, but they also reported committing more offenses. Men and women apologized for a similar share (~81%) of the offenses they themselves perceived. Men had a higher threshold for what counts as offensive.
- Correction: it does not show women "over-apologize"; it shows a different threshold.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797610384150 . VERIFIED (recall)

B2. Fawn response and refusing to apologize.
- Pete Walker coined "fawn" as the fourth F (fight/flight/freeze/fawn) in "Codependency, Trauma and the Fawn Response", The East Bay Therapist, Jan/Feb 2003. It was expanded in "Complex PTSD: From Surviving to Thriving" (2013). Caveat: it is a clinical construct, not an empirically validated diagnostic category. Source: https://www.pete-walker.com/codependencyFawnResponse.htm . VERIFIED (date per secondary sources)
- Okimoto, Wenzel & Hedrick (2013), European Journal of Social Psychology 43(1):22-31, "Refusing to apologize can have psychological benefits (and we issue no mea culpa for this research finding)." People who refused to apologize reported higher self-esteem, more feelings of power/control and more value integrity than people who apologized or who did not respond. Source: https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.1901 . VERIFIED (recall)
- People-pleasing: "sociotropy" (Beck, 1983) is linked to depression. There is limited direct research on chronic over-apologizing as such. PARTIAL

B3. Lewicki, Polin & Lount (2016).
- "An exploration of the structure of effective apologies", Negotiation and Conflict Management Research 9(2):177-196.
- Six elements: expression of regret, explanation, acknowledgment of responsibility, declaration of repentance, offer of repair, request for forgiveness.
- Acknowledgment of responsibility was rated most important, then offer of repair. Request for forgiveness was rated least important.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1111/ncmr.12073 . Ohio State news, 12 Apr 2016. VERIFIED (recall)

B4. Culture.
- Maddux, Kim, Okumura & Brett (2011), "Cultural differences in the function and meaning of apologies", International Negotiation 16(3):405-425. Japanese participants apologized more, including for things they were not responsible for; apology works as a way to repair relationships. Americans tie apology to admitting blame and personal responsibility. Source: https://doi.org/10.1163/157180611X592932 . VERIFIED (recall)
- Related: Guan, Park & Lee (2009, Int. J. Intercultural Relations) found Chinese and Korean participants felt less obligated than Americans to apologize to strangers, but similar or more to in-group members.
- India/Tamil: no peer-reviewed comparative apology study was found. Collectivist face/honour norms (e.g. "maanam") are plausible but unverified. NOT VERIFIED

## C. Does belief manifest?

C1. Placebo and nocebo.
- Kaptchuk et al. 2010, PLoS ONE 5(12):e15591. IBS patients (n=80) were openly told they were taking placebo pills. Adequate relief: 59% vs 35% for no-treatment controls at 3 weeks. Source: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0015591 . VERIFIED (recall)
- SAMSON trial: Wood et al., NEJM 2020;383:2182-84. 60 patients (49 completed), n-of-1 design with 12 months of statin, placebo and no tablet. The nocebo ratio was 0.90: 90% of the symptom burden on statins was also present on placebo. Source: https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMc2031173 . VERIFIED (recall)

C2. Optimism and mortality.
- Kim et al. 2017, American Journal of Epidemiology 185(1):21-29 (Nurses' Health Study, n=70,021, 2004-2012). Highest vs lowest optimism quartile: all-cause mortality HR 0.71 (~29% lower) after sociodemographic adjustment, and 0.91 after full adjustment for behaviours, health and depression. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5209589 . VERIFIED
- Lee et al. 2019, PNAS 116(37):18357-62 (NHS women plus VA Normative Aging Study men). The most optimistic group had an 11-15% longer lifespan, and 50-70% greater odds of reaching 85. Observational, so not causal. Source: https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1900712116 . VERIFIED (recall)

C3. Self-efficacy and mindset.
- Bandura 1977, "Self-efficacy: Toward a unifying theory of behavioral change", Psychological Review 84(2):191-215. VERIFIED (recall)
- Sisk et al. 2018, Psychological Science 29(4):549-571. Two meta-analyses: mindset-achievement r=.10; intervention effect d=0.08 overall, larger for low-SES and at-risk students. VERIFIED (recall)
- Yeager et al. 2019, Nature 573:364-369 (National Study of Learning Mindsets). n≈12,490 US 9th graders; a <1 hr online intervention raised GPA by ~0.10 points among lower-achieving students. VERIFIED (recall)
- Later debate: Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023 (Psych Bulletin) found effects near zero after correcting for bias. Burnette et al. 2023 (Psych Bulletin) found benefits for focal groups. DISPUTED

C4. Pygmalion effect.
- Rosenthal & Jacobson, "Pygmalion in the Classroom" (1968); the 1965-66 Oak School study.
- Critiques: Thorndike (1968), Snow (1969). Raudenbush's 1984 meta-analysis found effects only when expectations were induced in the first weeks of contact. Jussim & Harber 2005 (PSPR 9:131-155): effects are real but typically small (~0.1-0.2 SD), are larger for stigmatized groups, and often dissipate.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327957pspr0902_3 . PARTIAL/DISPUTED (real but small)

C5. Religious attendance and mortality.
- Correction: the first author is Shanshan Li (Li, Stampfer, Williams & VanderWeele), JAMA Internal Medicine 2016;176(6):777-785. Nurses' Health Study, n=74,534, 1996-2012.
- Attending more than once a week vs never: HR 0.67, i.e. 33% lower all-cause mortality. Once a week: HR 0.74 (26% lower). Less than weekly: HR 0.87.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2016.1615 . VERIFIED (recall)

C6. New Thought and law of attraction.
- Phineas Parkhurst Quimby (1802-1866), mesmerist and mental healer (Portland, Maine). Followers: Warren Felt Evans, Mary Baker Eddy (Christian Science, 1875 "Science and Health"; she later disputed Quimby's influence), Emma Curtis Hopkins.
- "Law of attraction" phrase: Blavatsky (1877) used it in an occult sense. It was popularised by Prentice Mulford and William Walker Atkinson ("Thought Vibration or the Law of Attraction", 1906), then Wallace Wattles ("The Science of Getting Rich", 1910).
- "The Secret" (Rhonda Byrne, film and book 2006): ~19 million copies by 2007; now commonly cited as ~30 million+ in ~50 languages (publisher bios say 34M+). Unaudited.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_(Byrne_book) . Mitch Horowitz, "One Simple Idea" (2014). PARTIAL (sales figure approximate)

C7. STEP (prayer study).
- Benson et al. 2006, American Heart Journal 151(4):934-42. n=1,802 CABG patients at 6 US hospitals.
- Complications: 52% for those told they might be prayed for and who were prayed for, vs 51% for those told they might be and were not. 59% for those told they would certainly be prayed for, vs 52%: significantly worse (RR 1.14).
- Your assumption is confirmed: no benefit, and the "certain" group did slightly worse.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ahj.2005.05.028 . VERIFIED (recall)

C8. Brown et al. 2010 (Mozambique).
- Candy Gunther Brown, Mory, Williams & McClymond, "Study of the therapeutic effects of proximal intercessory prayer (STEPP) on auditory and visual impairments in rural Mozambique", Southern Medical Journal 103(9):864-869.
- n=24 (14 hearing, 11 vision); pre/post measures with audiometer and eye charts. The improvements were significant (hearing p<0.003; vision p<0.02). 2 subjects improved hearing thresholds by ~50 dB; 3 went from ≤20/400 to ≥20/80. Prayer was given by Iris Ministries (Heidi Baker) pentecostals.
- Limitations: no control group, small sample, not blinded, practitioners were present. The authors allowed that placebo/suggestion may explain the results.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1097/SMJ.0b013e3181e8e8b5 . https://scholarworks.iu.edu/dspace/items/ad8f6faa-1a4f-4a8a-9638-2578c33c1f1e . VERIFIED

## D. Additional items

D1. Caste among Indian Christians, especially Tamil Nadu.
- Share of Dalits: commonly estimated at ~60-70% of Indian Christians. CSW/church sources say "two-thirds". Some Tamil Nadu Catholic estimates are higher (~65-70%). Census data cannot confirm this, because Dalit Christians are not counted as Scheduled Castes: the Presidential Order of 1950 limits SC status to Hindus, later extended to Sikhs (1956) and Buddhists (1990).
- Documented practices, mostly Tamil Nadu Catholic parishes:
  - separate seating, or Dalits seated at the back;
  - separate or walled-off cemeteries, and separate hearses;
  - Dalits receiving communion after others, or separate chalices (reported historically);
  - exclusion from festival processions through caste streets and from parish council or priestly roles;
  - separate churches.
- Notable conflict: Eraiyur (Villupuram), 2008, with clashes when Dalits demanded a separate parish.
- CBCI "Policy of Dalit Empowerment in the Catholic Church in India" (released 13 Dec 2016): "caste discrimination is a grave social sin"; "untouchability" "will not be tolerated". Dioceses were told to make plans within a year, "with particular attention to places of worship and burial grounds". Critics say implementation has been weak.
- Sources: Radio Vaticana, 15 Dec 2016. https://www.archivioradiovaticana.va/storico/2016/12/15/church_in_india_adopts_a_new_policy_towards_the_dalits/en-1279394 . The Tablet. https://www.thetablet.co.uk/news/6536/catholic-church-in-india-condemns-caste-system-in-new-policy-document-empowering-dalits . Human Rights Watch, "Broken People" (1999), ch. on Dalit Christians. https://www.hrw.org/reports/1999/india/ .
- Confidence: VERIFIED for the CBCI statement. PARTIAL for the percentage (estimate) and for the specific TN cases (recall; check the Eraiyur details before publishing).

D2. Gershoff & Grogan-Kaylor 2016.
- "Spanking and child outcomes: Old controversies and new meta-analyses", Journal of Family Psychology 30(4):453-469.
- Coverage: 75 studies (from 1961 to 2013), 111 effect sizes, 160,927 children.
- Findings:
  - Spanking (defined narrowly as open-hand hitting, not abuse) was significantly linked with 13 of 17 detrimental outcomes, including aggression, antisocial behaviour, mental-health problems, lower cognitive ability, lower self-esteem and a worse parent-child relationship.
  - It was not linked with any improved outcome, including immediate compliance; the compliance result was not significant.
  - Effects were similar to those of physical abuse, only slightly smaller.
- Source: https://doi.org/10.1037/fam0000191 . VERIFIED (recall)

D3. Judge Leon Bazile (Loving v. Virginia).
- Correction: the famous quote is NOT from the 6 Jan 1959 sentencing. It comes from Bazile's 22 Jan 1965 opinion denying the Lovings' motion to vacate, and the U.S. Supreme Court quoted it in 1967 (388 U.S. 1, at 3).
- Quote: "Almighty God created the races white, black, yellow, malay and red, and he placed them on separate continents. And but for the interference with his arrangement there would be no cause for such marriages. The fact that he separated the races shows that he did not intend for the races to mix."
- The 1959 sentence was 1 year in jail, suspended for 25 years on condition that they leave Virginia.
- Source: https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/388/1/ . VERIFIED (recall)

D4. Germantown Quaker Petition (1688).
- Date: 18 Feb 1688 (Old Style). Signed by Francis Daniel Pastorius, Garret Hendericks, Derick op den Graeff and Abraham op den Graeff, Dutch/German Quakers in Germantown, PA. It is the first known written protest against slavery in the English American colonies.
- Its argument from the Golden Rule: "there is a saying, that we shall doe to all men like as we will be done ourselves; making no difference of what generation, descent or colour they are."
- Monthly, Quarterly and Yearly Meetings passed it up the chain without acting. It was rediscovered in 1844. Philadelphia Yearly Meeting did not bar slaveholding members until 1776.
- Source: Haverford College Quaker & Special Collections (original MS). https://www.haverford.edu/library/special-collections . VERIFIED (recall)

D5. Isaiah 7:9b wordplay.
- Hebrew: אִם לֹא תַאֲמִינוּ כִּי לֹא תֵאָמֵנוּ ('im lo' ta'aminu ki lo' te'amenu).
- Both verbs come from the root '-m-n (aman, "firm/reliable", the root of "amen"). Ta'aminu is Hiphil, "believe / stand firm in trust". Te'amenu is Niphal, "be made firm / established / endure".
- Translations: NRSV "If you do not stand firm in faith, you shall not stand at all." KJV "If ye will not believe, surely ye shall not be established."
- Context: it is addressed to King Ahaz (the house of David, plural "you") during the Syro-Ephraimite threat (c. 735-734 BC). It is about trusting God's promise rather than military alliances, not a general law that belief creates outcomes. The LXX renders the second verb as "understand" (oude me syneite), the source of Augustine's "nisi credideritis, non intelligetis."
