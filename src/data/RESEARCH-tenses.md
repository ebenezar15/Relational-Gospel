## A. Origins of the triad and the term

1. **Triad "penalty / power / presence (of sin)": origin.** No 19th-c. source found that clearly coins it. The earliest *dated* hit is George W. Noble, *Book of 750 Bible and Gospel Studies* (Chicago, 1909), as reproduced by bible.org. Plymouth Brethren writer George Cutting (1834–1934), in *Freedom from Sin: What Is It?*, has "its penalty, judicially; its power, practically; its presence, absolutely" (date of pamphlet not confirmed). Spurgeon (1 Nov 1885, Rom 6:23 sermon) has only the penalty/dominion pair, with no "presence." No Moody or Keswick coinage found. | bible.org/illustration/salvation-three-tenses ; bibletruthpublishers.com (Cutting) ; ccel.org/ccel/spurgeon/sermons31.liii.html | **NOT VERIFIED** (who popularized it) / **PARTIAL** (Brethren and early-1900s fundamentalist circulation)

2. **Scofield Reference Bible note on Rom 1:16** (1909; text online is the 1917 revision). Salvation in three tenses: the believer "has been saved from the guilt and penalty of sin ... and is safe"; "is being saved from the habit and dominion of sin"; "is to be saved ... [in] entire conformity to Christ." It says *guilt/penalty → habit/dominion → conformity*. It does **not** say "presence." | blueletterbible.org/scofield-reference-bible/notes/rom/chapter-1 | 1917 | **VERIFIED** for the 1917 text; 1909 wording PARTIAL

3. **Scholarly "three tenses" in Paul.** A.M. Hunter: "When Paul thought about Christian salvation, he saw it as a word with three tenses: a past event, a present experience, and a future hope" (*The Gospel According to Paul*, 1966, p. 15). Colijn quotes this. | Colijn, "The Three Tenses of Salvation in Paul's Letters," *Ashland Theological Journal* 22 (1990): 29–41 | gospelstudies.org.uk/biblicalstudies/pdf/ashland_theological_journal/22-1_29.pdf | **VERIFIED** (via Colijn)

4. **"Ordo salutis" coinage.** Schaff-Herzog (1910): the phrase "seems to occur for the first time in Buddeus (*Institutiones theologiae dogmaticae*, p. 40, Leipsic, 1723)." Library catalogs also list a 1724 Fritsch printing. Jacob Carpov is often credited with 1737, but the catalog date found for his *Theologia revelata dogmatica* is 1737–1739 (vol. 2 is 1739). Wikipedia says "mid-1720s." **Correction:** the Buddeus date is 1723 (first ed.), not 1724, and Buddeus comes before Carpov, so Carpov is not the coiner. | ccel.org/ccel/schaff/encyc08/htm/ii.vii.htm ; en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordo_salutis | **PARTIAL** (Buddeus 1723 rests on Schaff-Herzog's hedged "seems")

## B. Reformed debates

5. **WCF 11.1 wording.** "...by imputing the obedience and satisfaction of Christ unto them, they receiving and resting on him and his righteousness by faith..." The confession does not use the words "active"/"passive" or "whole obedience." | WCF text (puritanboard quotation; standard text) | 1646 | **VERIFIED**

6. **Westminster Assembly active-obedience debate.** Took place in September 1643, during the revision of Article 11 of the Thirty-Nine Articles. The committee proposed "whole obedience." Thomas Gataker led a minority that moved to strike "whole," with Richard Vines his chief ally; their concern was that the formula weakened sanctification. William Twisse (prolocutor) is reported as sympathetic but largely silent; Van Dixhoorn (2009) treats his "silence." The main sources are Van Dixhoorn's Cambridge PhD (2004) and *Minutes and Papers of the Westminster Assembly* (OUP 2012). WCF 11 (1646) is widely read as a compromise. **Correction:** the date is 1643, not 1643–46, and Twisse should not be listed as a vocal dissenter. | Kirk, *SBET* 24.1 ; aspiringapollos.com (2026) ; cambridge.org JEH | **PARTIAL** (Gataker/Vines VERIFIED; Twisse's position PARTIAL)

7. **Piscator (1546–1625)** limited imputation to Christ's passive obedience (satisfaction). French national synods opposed him (Gap 1603; La Rochelle 1607; Privas 1612 required ministers to affirm imputation of active righteousness). Heber Carlos de Campos, *Doctrine in Development: Johannes Piscator and Debates over Christ's Active Obedience* (RHB, 2017). The Irish Articles (1615) explicitly affirmed active obedience. | en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Piscator ; heritagebooks.org ; ccel Schaff-Herzog | **VERIFIED** (synod years PARTIAL)

8. **John Murray, "Definitive Sanctification,"** *Calvin Theological Journal* 2.1 (April 1967): 5–21. Reprinted in *Collected Writings* 2:277–93. Thesis: the NT's most characteristic sanctification language denotes a once-for-all definitive break (Rom 6), not only a process. | heidelblog.net/2009/08/what-is-definitive-sanctification-and-is-it-reformed/ | 1967 | **VERIFIED** (page range 5–21 from memory, PARTIAL)

## D. pistis Christou

- ESV: footnote "Or through the faithfulness of."
- NIV/NASB/NKJV: "faith in" in the text (NIV 2011 has a footnote "faithfulness of").

## E. Greek tense data

17. **sōzō** occurs **106×** in the NT (Mounce; diasōzō 8× separately). **sōtēria** occurs **46×** (Abarim/standard concordances), not 45. **Correction:** use 46. Counts vary by text edition. | billmounce.com/greek-dictionary/sozo ; abarim-publications.com | 106 VERIFIED; 46 PARTIAL

18. **Tense distribution in Paul** (Colijn 1990):
- In the undisputed letters, only *one* past-tense (aorist) sōzō: Rom 8:24 "in this hope we were saved." Even that verse is forward-looking.
- Perfect: Eph 2:5, 2:8 ("by grace you have been saved"). Colijn calls these "the only two instances of salvation in the perfect tense in the New Testament." **That is wrong for the whole NT:** the Gospels have the perfect *sesōken* ("your faith has saved you": Mark 5:34; 10:52; Luke 7:50; 8:48; 17:19; 18:42). It holds only for the epistles.
- Present: 1 Cor 1:18 and 2 Cor 2:15 (*tois sōzomenois*, "being saved"), which are corporate; Phil 2:12 "work out your salvation."
- Future dominates: Rom 5:9–10 "shall be saved from wrath ... through his life"; Rom 13:11 "salvation is nearer now than when we first believed."
- Foerster (TDNT 7:992): "Primarily, then, sōtēria is for Paul a future, eschatological term." TDNT 7:993: sōzomai/sōtēria, "in contrast to justification, reconciliation and redemption, refer to future, eschatological salvation."
- Other past forms outside the undisputed letters: 2 Tim 1:9 and Titus 3:5 (aorist "he saved us").
| Colijn, ATJ 22 (1990) | **VERIFIED** (Colijn's data); her "only two in NT" claim **DISPUTED**

19. **Implication for the critique:** Paul's own *sōzō* usage is predominantly future. "Saved" = justification (past) is a systematic mapping, not Paul's word usage. Paul's past-tense term is chiefly *dikaioō* (justified; Rom 5:1, 9). | Colijn ; Foerster | **VERIFIED** (as scholarly observation)

## F. Hebrew vocabulary

20. **Exod 34:7** "forgiving iniquity (*ʿāwōn*) and transgression (*pešaʿ*) and sin (*ḥaṭṭāʾâ*)." **Correction:** the word in Exod 34:7 is *chatta'ah* (Strong's 2403, ~290×), not *chet* (Strong's 2399, ~33×). The root *ḥ-ṭ-ʾ* means "to miss (the mark)" (cf. Judg 20:16). Meanings: *pesha* = rebellion/breach of relationship; *avon* = iniquity, twistedness, also the guilt or punishment that results. **Ps 32:1–2:** v1 has *pesha* + *chata'ah*, v2 has *avon*, so the three are spread across two verses. Ps 32:5 has all three in one verse. The Talmud reads the three terms in Exod 34:7 as distinct (Yoma 36b). | tanakh.info/ex34-7 ; etzion.org.il/en/node/5310 ; ibri.org PDF | **VERIFIED**

21. ***teshuvah*** (תשובה, Strong's 8666): BDB "return, answer." The sense "repentance" comes later (rabbinic); the verb is *shuv* "to turn/return." | biblehub BDB 8666 ; chabad.org | **VERIFIED**
- ***yeshuah*** (ישועה, Strong's **3444**, ~78×): salvation, deliverance, victory, often physical/historical rescue. Do not confuse with *teshuʿah* (8668), a related word.
- ***tzedakah*** (צדקה): righteousness/justice, i.e. right conduct within a relationship or covenant; "charity" is a later rabbinic sense.
- ***kadosh*** (קדוש): holy, set apart/consecrated.
| standard lexica (BDB/HALOT) | PARTIAL (lexicon pages not fetched; glosses standard)

## Corrections summary
- Ordo salutis: Buddeus 1723 (not 1724) is the earliest attestation; Carpov is later (1737–39), not the coiner.
- Scofield's note says "guilt and penalty / habit and dominion / conformity to Christ," not "presence."
- The source of the triad is unknown. It is 19th–early 20th c., with Brethren (Cutting) and popular sources by 1909; no Moody or Keswick coinage found.
- Westminster debate: September 1643 (Article 11 revision). Gataker and Vines were the dissenters; Twisse should not be listed as a vocal dissenter.
- sōtēria = 46× (not 45). sōzō 106× confirmed.
- Exod 34:7 uses *chatta'ah*, not *chet*. Ps 32:1–2 spreads the three terms over two verses; Ps 32:5 has all three.
- *yeshuah* = Strong's 3444 (not 8668).
- NRSVue (2021) has "faith of Jesus Christ" in the TEXT (reversing NRSV 1989). CEB has "faithfulness of" in the text.
- Paul uses *sōzō* mostly in the future. There is only one aorist in the undisputed letters (Rom 8:24). The Gospels do have perfects, which contradicts Colijn's "only Eph 2:5, 8."
- "Victorious life" is the American (Trumbull, 1913+) label, not the 1875 Keswick term.
